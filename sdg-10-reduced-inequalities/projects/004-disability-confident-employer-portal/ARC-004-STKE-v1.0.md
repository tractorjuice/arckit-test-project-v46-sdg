## Executive Summary

### Purpose

This document identifies key stakeholders for the Disability Confident Employer Portal, their underlying drivers, how these manifest into goals, and measurable outcomes.

### Key Findings

The Disability Confident Employer Portal faces a credibility challenge: the current scheme has been criticised by disability charities and the Work and Pensions Committee for being too easy to achieve without meaningful action. Over 19,000 employers are signed up, but the disability employment gap (difference between employment rates for disabled and non-disabled people) remains at approximately 28 percentage points. The platform must transform the scheme from a self-assessment badge into an evidence-based certification with measurable outcomes, while retaining employer engagement. The central tension is between DWP's desire to increase employer participation (quantity) and disability charities' demand for rigorous assessment that genuinely changes employer practices (quality).

### Critical Success Factors

- Digitise and streamline the Disability Confident accreditation workflow, reducing processing time from 8 weeks to 2 weeks
- Implement evidence-based assessment for Level 2 (Disability Confident Employer) and Level 3 (Disability Confident Leader) replacing pure self-assessment
- Integrate with DWP Access to Work data to provide employers with seamless referral pathways
- Track the disability employment gap at employer and sector level to measure scheme effectiveness
- Maintain current employer participation (19,000+) while raising quality standards

### Stakeholder Alignment Score

**Overall Alignment**: LOW

Fundamental disagreement between DWP (maximise participation, voluntary scheme), disability charities (rigorous assessment, mandatory elements), employers (minimal administrative burden), and disabled jobseekers (genuine change in hiring practices). The portal redesign must navigate these competing interests.

## Stakeholder Drivers Analysis

### SD-1: DWP Minister — Close the Disability Employment Gap

**Stakeholder**: DWP Minister for Disabled People, Health and Work

**Driver Category**: STRATEGIC

**Driver Statement**: Use the Disability Confident scheme as a flagship policy instrument to close the disability employment gap, demonstrating that voluntary employer action, supported by government, can improve employment outcomes for disabled people.

**Context & Background**:
The disability employment gap stands at approximately 28 percentage points (52% vs 80% employment rate). The government has committed to halving this gap. The current Disability Confident scheme has 19,000+ employer sign-ups but limited evidence of impact on actual hiring practices. The Minister needs the portal to transform the scheme into a credible, measurable intervention.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Digital platform enabling data collection on employer practices and outcomes
- Integration with DWP labour market data to track employment outcomes
- Employer recognition and incentives for genuine progress

**Blockers**:

- Employer resistance to increased reporting burden
- Difficulty attributing employment outcomes to scheme participation
- Political sensitivity around mandatory vs voluntary approaches

---

### SD-2: Scope — Genuine Behaviour Change, Not Badging

**Stakeholder**: Scope

**Driver Category**: CUSTOMER

**Driver Statement**: Transform Disability Confident from a self-assessment badge that employers display without meaningful action into an evidence-based certification that requires demonstrable changes in recruitment practices, workplace adjustments, and retention of disabled employees.

**Context & Background**:
Scope's research found that many Disability Confident employers still have inaccessible recruitment processes, fail to offer reasonable adjustments, and show no improvement in disability employment rates after joining the scheme. The Work and Pensions Committee called the scheme "too easy to pass" in its 2024 report. Scope wants external validation of employer claims.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Evidence-based assessment criteria replacing pure self-assessment
- Disabled employees' feedback integrated into employer assessment
- Public reporting of employer progress metrics
- Consequences for non-compliance (downgrading, removal from scheme)

**Blockers**:

- DWP reluctance to make scheme more onerous (fears employer dropout)
- Employer resistance to external scrutiny of HR practices
- Cost of external assessment visits

---

### SD-3: CBI / FSB — Minimal Administrative Burden

**Stakeholder**: CBI (Confederation of British Industry) and FSB (Federation of Small Businesses)

**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL

**Driver Statement**: Ensure the portal minimises administrative burden on employers, particularly SMEs, by digitising the application process, pre-populating data from existing sources, and avoiding duplicative reporting requirements.

**Context & Background**:
The current scheme application involves PDF forms, email submissions, and 6-8 week processing times. Employers (especially SMEs with limited HR resource) want a streamlined digital process. The CBI and FSB will resist any changes that significantly increase employer reporting burden, particularly if they come without clear business benefit.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Digital self-service portal with guided application workflow
- Pre-populated data from Companies House, PAYE records
- Tiered requirements (simpler for Level 1, more evidence for Levels 2-3)
- Clear business benefit articulation (talent pipeline, public procurement advantage)

**Blockers**:

- Disability charities' demands for rigorous evidence may increase burden
- Data integration with HMRC/Companies House requires cross-government agreements
- SMEs may see any evidence requirement as excessive

---

### SD-4: Business Disability Forum — Alignment with Disability Smart

**Stakeholder**: Business Disability Forum (BDF)

**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL

**Driver Statement**: Align the Disability Confident portal with BDF's Disability Smart benchmarking framework to avoid employer confusion from competing assessment schemes, and integrate the strengths of both approaches (government reach + BDF depth).

**Context & Background**:
BDF operates the Disability Smart certification, which is more rigorous than Disability Confident but has far fewer participants (~500 vs 19,000). Some large employers hold both certifications and report duplication. A aligned or integrated approach would reduce employer burden and strengthen both schemes.

**Driver Intensity**: MEDIUM

**Enablers**:

- Joint assessment framework development
- Mutual recognition of evidence (pass Disability Smart = meet Disability Confident Level 3 evidence requirements)
- Shared employer reporting portal

**Blockers**:

- BDF has commercial interest in maintaining a separate premium offer
- Methodology differences between schemes
- DWP ownership of Disability Confident vs BDF independence

---

### SD-5: Disabled Jobseekers — Transparent Employer Information

**Stakeholder**: Disabled jobseekers and employees

**Driver Category**: CUSTOMER

**Driver Statement**: Access reliable, honest information about employers' disability inclusion practices and track records, enabling informed job application decisions, rather than relying on a badge that may not reflect reality.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Public employer profiles showing specific inclusion practices
- Employee feedback/review mechanism (anonymised)
- Reasonable adjustment provisions clearly listed
- Integration with DWP Find a Job to show Disability Confident status

**Blockers**:

- Employers may resist public scrutiny of HR practices
- Risk of gaming or misleading self-reported data
- Privacy concerns around employer-level disability employment data

### Conflict Analysis

**Competing Drivers**:

- **Conflict 1**: Scope (SD-2) wants rigorous evidence-based assessment, but CBI/FSB (SD-3) wants minimal administrative burden. Adding evidence requirements increases employer work.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Tiered approach — Level 1 remains light-touch self-declaration (entry point), Level 2 requires moderate evidence (uploaded documents, employee survey), Level 3 requires comprehensive evidence (external validation, outcome data). SMEs have simplified evidence requirements.

- **Conflict 2**: DWP Minister (SD-1) wants to maintain/grow 19,000+ employer participation, but raising quality standards (SD-2) risks significant dropout.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Grandfather existing Level 1 employers for 12-month transition. New evidence requirements phased in for Level 2-3 renewals. Support programme for employers struggling to meet new standards. Public procurement incentives to maintain motivation.

**Synergies**:

- **Synergy 1**: Digital accreditation (SD-3) and evidence-based assessment (SD-2) are complementary — a digital platform can collect evidence efficiently, reducing the burden of demonstrating compliance
- **Synergy 2**: Disabled jobseekers' need for transparent information (SD-5) and Scope's demand for genuine behaviour change (SD-2) both benefit from public employer profiles with verified data

## Risk Register (Stakeholder-Related)

### Risk R-1: Mass Employer Dropout

**Related Stakeholders**: DWP Minister, CBI/FSB, employers

**Risk Description**: Introduction of evidence-based assessment causes a significant proportion of the 19,000+ employers to leave the scheme rather than provide evidence, undermining the scheme's scale and political credibility.

**Probability**: MEDIUM | **Impact**: HIGH

**Mitigation Strategy**: Phased introduction with 12-month transition. Level 1 remains light-touch. Support programme for employers. Public procurement incentives to maintain engagement. Monitor dropout rates monthly and adjust timeline if needed.

---

### Risk R-2: Disability Charities Reject Reforms as Insufficient

**Related Stakeholders**: Scope, Mencap, RNIB, Disability Rights UK

**Risk Description**: Disability charities publicly criticise the reformed scheme as still inadequate, particularly if evidence requirements are watered down to retain employer participation, undermining the portal's credibility with disabled people.

**Probability**: MEDIUM | **Impact**: MEDIUM

**Mitigation Strategy**: Co-design evidence criteria with disability charities. Establish Disability Sector Advisory Panel with formal input to scheme design. Commit to publishing employer-level outcome data.
