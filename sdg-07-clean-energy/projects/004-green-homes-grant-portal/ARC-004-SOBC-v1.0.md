## Executive Summary

**Purpose**: The UK's building decarbonisation programme requires a robust digital platform to manage £1 billion+ annual grant funding across multiple home energy efficiency schemes. The 2020 Green Homes Grant scheme failure (NAO: "botched") demonstrated that administrative systems, not policy intent, determine programme success.

**Problem Statement**: Multiple grant schemes (BUS, HUG, LADS, ECO4) operate on disparate systems with manual processes, inconsistent eligibility checking, limited fraud detection, and no outcome tracking. The 2020 scheme failed to disburse 91% of allocated funding. Without a unified digital platform, the Net Zero building decarbonisation target of 600,000 heat pump installations/year by 2028 is unachievable.

**Proposed Solution**: Build a unified grant management portal handling applications, eligibility verification, installer management, payment processing, fraud detection, and outcome tracking for all home energy efficiency schemes.

**Investment Required**: £18M over 3 years
- Capital: £12M
- Operational (3 years): £6M

**Expected Benefits**: £52M over 5 years
- Administrative efficiency: £15M (reduced manual processing across local authorities)
- Fraud prevention: £20M (reducing fraud rate from 5-7% to <2% on £1B+ annual grants)
- Carbon reduction value: £12M (2 MtCO2e/year at £30/tonne social cost)
- Improved policy targeting: £5M (better data enables more effective grant allocation)

**Return on Investment**:
- NPV: £23.8M (discounted at 3.5%)
- Payback Period: 18 months
- ROI: 189%

**Recommended Option**: Option 2: Unified Multi-Scheme Platform

**Go/No-Go Recommendation**: **PROCEED**

# PART A: STRATEGIC CASE

## A1. Strategic Context

### A1.1 Problem Statement

**Current Situation**: Home energy efficiency grants are administered through a patchwork of systems. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme uses one portal, HUG uses local authority spreadsheets, LADS varies by council, and ECO4 is managed by energy suppliers through Ofgem. There is no unified view of grant spend, no automated eligibility checking, no systematic fraud detection, and no mechanism to track whether funded improvements actually reduce energy consumption.

**Consequences of Inaction**:
- £50-70M annual fraud losses on £1B+ grant budget (5-7% rate based on comparable government schemes)
- 91% funding underspend risk (as demonstrated in 2020 scheme)
- Net Zero 2028 heat pump target missed — insufficient administrative capacity to process 600,000 installations/year
- Inability to demonstrate carbon reduction outcomes to justify continued funding

### A1.2 Strategic Alignment

- **Net Zero Strategy**: 600,000 heat pump installations/year by 2028
- **Heat and Buildings Strategy**: Comprehensive building retrofit programme
- **Energy Act 2023**: Powers for building efficiency improvements
- **Levelling Up White Paper**: Fuel poverty reduction through energy efficiency

**Description**: Upgrade individual scheme portals (BUS, HUG) independently. Add basic fraud checks. No unification.
**Costs (3-year)**: Capital £6M; Operational £4M; Total £10M

# PART E: MANAGEMENT CASE

## E1. Delivery Approach

**Phases**:
1. **Discovery** (Months 1-3): User research with householders, installers, local authorities; 2020 lessons learned; fraud pattern analysis
2. **Alpha** (Months 4-7): Application portal prototype, eligibility engine POC, fraud detection spike
