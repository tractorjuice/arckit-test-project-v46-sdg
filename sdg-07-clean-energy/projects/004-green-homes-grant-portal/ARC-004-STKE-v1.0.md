## Executive Summary

### Purpose

This document identifies stakeholders for the Green Homes Grant Portal — the digital application and management system for government home energy efficiency grants, including heat pump installations, insulation, glazing, and other retrofit measures. The portal must handle £1 billion+ in annual grant funding across multiple schemes (Green Homes Grant, Boiler Upgrade Scheme, Home Upgrade Grant, ECO4).

### Key Findings

The Green Homes Grant programme has a troubled history — the original 2020 scheme was abandoned after 6 months due to administrative failings, contractor availability, and an overwhelmed application system. Stakeholder trust is fragile. The strongest alignment is between DESNZ's Net Zero building targets and householders' desire to reduce energy costs through home improvements. The key tension is between application volume management (preventing system overload) and the political need to demonstrate rapid grant disbursement. Local authorities occupy a critical delivery role but face capacity constraints. The installer supply chain is a binding constraint — grant availability is meaningless without qualified installers.

### Critical Success Factors

- Process 500,000 grant applications per year without the system failures that plagued the 2020 scheme
- Disburse grants within 30 days of approved work completion (Treasury condition)
- Integrate with the EPC register to validate eligibility and measure improvement outcomes
- Support multiple concurrent grant schemes with different eligibility criteria and funding sources
- Prevent fraud while maintaining a user-friendly application process

### Stakeholder Alignment Score

**Overall Alignment**: MEDIUM

Strong alignment on the need for a robust grant portal. Residual distrust from the 2020 scheme failure. Tensions between rapid disbursement (political urgency), fraud prevention (NAO concern), installer capacity (supply constraint), and local authority delivery burden (resource constraints).

## Stakeholder Drivers Analysis

### SD-1: DESNZ Secretary of State — Building Decarbonisation Delivery

**Stakeholder**: Secretary of State for Energy Security and Net Zero
**Driver Category**: STRATEGIC
**Driver Statement**: Demonstrate that government can successfully deliver home energy efficiency grant programmes at scale, overcoming the reputational damage of the 2020 Green Homes Grant scheme failure. The Net Zero Strategy commits to 600,000 heat pump installations per year by 2028 — grant programmes are a primary delivery mechanism.
**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

### SD-2: NAO — Value for Money and Fraud Prevention

**Stakeholder**: National Audit Office
**Driver Category**: COMPLIANCE
**Driver Statement**: Ensure that grant funding delivers genuine energy efficiency improvements without fraud, waste, or administrative failure. The NAO's 2021 report on the Green Homes Grant was highly critical, finding that 91% of allocated funding was unspent and the scheme was "botched."
**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

### SD-3: Householders — Affordable Home Energy Improvements

**Stakeholder**: Householders (particularly fuel-poor)
**Driver Category**: CUSTOMER
**Driver Statement**: Access grant funding to reduce home energy costs through insulation, heat pumps, and other improvements. Average householder cannot afford £10,000+ for a heat pump or £8,000 for external wall insulation without grant support. The application process must be simple, transparent, and not require technical knowledge.
**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

### SD-4: Local Authorities — Delivery Capacity

**Stakeholder**: 152 upper-tier local authorities
**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL
**Driver Statement**: Deliver Home Upgrade Grant (HUG) and Local Authority Delivery Scheme (LADS) programmes within allocated budgets and timescales, despite limited staff capacity and competing priorities. Local authorities want a digital system that reduces their administrative burden, not one that adds to it.
**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

### SD-5: Installers — Business Pipeline and Certification

**Stakeholder**: MCS-certified heat pump and insulation installers
**Driver Category**: FINANCIAL
**Driver Statement**: Receive a steady pipeline of grant-funded work with prompt payment. The 2020 scheme failure left installers with unpaid work and abandoned installations. Trust must be rebuilt. Installers need a system that confirms grant approval before work begins and pays promptly after completion.
**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

## Driver-to-Goal Mapping

### Goal G-1: Robust Grant Application Platform

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-1, SD-2, SD-3
**Goal Owner**: SRO
**Goal Statement**: Process 500,000 grant applications per year with 99.9% system availability, <48 hour eligibility determination, and <30 day payment after approved work completion, by Q2 2027.

### Goal G-2: Fraud Prevention Without Friction

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-2, SD-3
**Goal Owner**: DESNZ Counter-Fraud Team
**Goal Statement**: Implement automated fraud detection (duplicate applications, installer gaming, inflated quotes) that identifies 95% of fraudulent applications without adding more than 2 days to genuine applicant processing time, by Q2 2027.

### Goal G-3: Multi-Scheme Management

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-1, SD-4
**Goal Owner**: DESNZ Building Decarbonisation Division
**Goal Statement**: Support concurrent operation of 4+ grant schemes (Green Homes Grant, Boiler Upgrade Scheme, HUG, ECO4) with different eligibility criteria, funding sources, and delivery models on a single platform, by Q2 2027.

### Goal G-4: Outcome Measurement

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-1, SD-2
**Goal Owner**: DESNZ Chief Analyst
**Goal Statement**: Track the energy efficiency impact of every grant-funded installation by linking to EPC before/after ratings and smart meter consumption data, providing evidence of carbon reduction, by Q4 2027.

## Conflict Analysis

- **Conflict 1**: Rapid grant disbursement (SD-1) vs. thorough fraud prevention (SD-2). Ministers want to announce high disbursement numbers; NAO wants rigorous controls that may slow processing.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Risk-based fraud detection — automated checks for the majority of applications (fast track); enhanced manual review only for flagged applications (10-15% of total). Proportionate controls that satisfy both speed and integrity requirements.

- **Conflict 2**: Householder simplicity (SD-3) vs. evidence requirements (SD-2, SD-4). Householders want a simple form; local authorities and auditors need detailed evidence of eligibility and work completion.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Progressive disclosure — simple front-end for householders with automated data gathering from EPC register, benefits databases (DWP), and council tax records to verify eligibility without requiring householders to provide extensive documentation.
