## Executive Summary

### Purpose

This document identifies stakeholders for the Community Energy Fund Tracker — a digital platform for managing community renewable energy project funding, tracking generation output, monitoring social and environmental impact, and reporting on community benefit disbursement. The platform supports the Community Energy Fund, Rural Community Energy Fund, and local authority community energy programmes.

### Key Findings

Community energy occupies a unique position in the energy transition — it combines grassroots engagement, local economic benefit, and renewable generation. The strongest alignment exists between DESNZ's desire to scale community energy (currently only 1% of UK renewable capacity) and community groups' desire for simplified administration. The key tension is between the need for proportionate governance (these are small organisations managing public money) and the reality that volunteer-run community energy organisations have limited administrative capacity. The platform must be sophisticated enough for DESNZ reporting but simple enough for a community group treasurer using it once a month.

### Critical Success Factors

- Manage 1,000+ active community energy projects with total funding of £200M+
- Reduce project reporting burden by 60% through automated data collection (generation metering, financial reporting)
- Track community benefit disbursement (fuel poverty payments, education programmes, local reinvestment)
- Demonstrate the social and environmental return on community energy investment to maintain political support

### Stakeholder Alignment Score

**Overall Alignment**: HIGH

Strong consensus that community energy needs better administrative infrastructure. Community groups want simpler processes; DESNZ wants better data. The main risk is that the platform becomes over-engineered for its audience — volunteer-run organisations that value simplicity above all else.

## Stakeholder Drivers Analysis

### SD-1: DESNZ — Scale Community Energy Contribution

**Stakeholder**: DESNZ Community Energy Policy
**Driver Category**: STRATEGIC
**Driver Statement**: Scale community energy from 1% to 5% of UK renewable capacity by 2030, demonstrating that local ownership of energy generation builds public support for the Net Zero transition and delivers measurable community benefit. Community energy has bipartisan political support and generates social value beyond carbon reduction — but needs better administrative infrastructure to scale.
**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

### SD-2: Community Energy Organisations — Administrative Simplicity

**Stakeholder**: 300+ community energy groups
**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL
**Driver Statement**: Reduce the administrative burden of managing government-funded community energy projects. Most community energy organisations are volunteer-run with limited professional capacity. Current reporting requirements consume 20-30% of volunteer time. Groups want to spend their time building solar farms and running community benefit programmes, not filling in spreadsheets.
**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

### SD-3: Community Energy England — Sector Growth and Visibility

**Stakeholder**: Community Energy England
**Driver Category**: STRATEGIC
**Driver Statement**: Demonstrate the sector's impact through aggregated data on generation, community benefit, and carbon reduction. CEE needs data to make the case for continued and increased government support. Currently, sector-wide data relies on an annual volunteer survey with <50% response rate.
**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

### SD-4: Rural Communities — Local Energy Benefits

**Stakeholder**: Rural communities hosting community energy projects
**Driver Category**: CUSTOMER
**Driver Statement**: See tangible local benefits from community energy projects — reduced energy bills for fuel-poor households, community fund payments for local facilities, and jobs in renewable energy maintenance. Communities granted planning permission for wind turbines and solar farms want visible evidence of the promised community benefits.
**Driver Intensity**: MEDIUM

## Driver-to-Goal Mapping

### Goal G-1: Unified Project and Fund Management

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-1, SD-2
**Goal Owner**: SRO
**Goal Statement**: Provide a single platform managing all community energy fund applications, grant disbursements, and project reporting for 1,000+ projects, reducing project reporting time by 60% by Q2 2028.

### Goal G-2: Automated Generation Tracking

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-1, SD-3
**Goal Owner**: DESNZ Community Energy Policy
**Goal Statement**: Automatically collect generation data from community energy installations via smart meter or inverter feeds, eliminating manual generation reporting for 80% of projects by Q4 2027.

### Goal G-3: Community Benefit Tracking

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-1, SD-3, SD-4
**Goal Owner**: CEE
**Goal Statement**: Track and publish community benefit disbursement (fuel poverty payments, education spending, local reinvestment) for all funded projects, with public-facing impact dashboard, by Q2 2028.

## Conflict Analysis

- **Conflict 1**: DESNZ needs robust reporting and accountability (SD-1) but community groups want minimal administration (SD-2).
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Automate data collection wherever possible (generation metering, financial APIs); require only exception reporting from community groups; proportionate reporting for small projects (<£50K) vs. large projects (>£500K).
