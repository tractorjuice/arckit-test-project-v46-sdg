## Document Purpose

This SOBC presents the case for investing in a Climate Adaptation Planning Tool for local authorities, following HM Treasury Green Book five-case model methodology.

---

## Executive Summary

**Purpose**: Local authorities are responsible for local climate adaptation but most lack the tools, capacity, and expertise to develop effective adaptation plans. This project will deliver a free, accessible digital tool enabling local authorities to create evidence-based adaptation plans using risk data from the Climate Risk Assessment Platform (Project 002).

**Problem Statement**: The CCC rates local adaptation planning as "insufficient" — only 28 of 152 upper-tier authorities in England have adaptation plans, and most are of poor quality. Local authority climate teams typically comprise 0.5-2 FTEs responsible for both mitigation and adaptation. Creating an adaptation plan from scratch takes approximately 6 months of officer time that most authorities cannot spare.

**Proposed Solution**: Build a guided, template-based adaptation planning tool pre-populated with locally relevant risk data from Project 002, with structured outputs enabling CCC national assessment.

**Strategic Fit**: Directly delivers NAP3 commitment to "provide tools and evidence to support local adaptation" and addresses CCC's repeatedly identified local adaptation gap.

**Investment Required**: GBP 5.8M over 3 years

- Capital: GBP 3.5M
- Operational (3 years): GBP 2.3M

**Expected Benefits**: GBP 14.0M over 5 years

- Avoided local climate damage through better adaptation: GBP 8.5M (based on EA evidence of adaptation benefit ratios)
- Local authority time savings: GBP 3.0M
- Improved CCC national assessment capability: GBP 1.5M (qualitative, enabling value)
- Reduced central government support costs: GBP 1.0M

**Return on Investment**:

- NPV: GBP 5.8M (discounted at 3.5%)
- Payback Period: 22 months
- ROI: 141%

**Recommended Option**: Option 2: Guided Adaptation Planning Tool with Risk Integration

**Key Risks**:

1. Local authority adoption insufficient — mitigated by LGA partnership, free tool, minimal training
2. Project 002 data not available on schedule — mitigated by standalone functionality with manual data entry fallback
3. Elected members do not prioritise adaptation — mitigated by committee-ready reports showing local risk evidence

**Go/No-Go Recommendation**: **PROCEED**

**Rationale**: The CCC has identified local adaptation as a critical gap for five consecutive years. The cost of the tool (GBP 5.8M) is negligible compared to the GBP 1.2 billion annual cost of climate-related damage to local infrastructure. The Environment Agency estimates that every GBP 1 spent on adaptation planning yields GBP 6 in avoided damage. The tool directly delivers a NAP3 commitment and addresses a statutory advisory body's repeated criticism.

# PART A: STRATEGIC CASE

## A1. Strategic Context

### A1.1 Problem Statement

**Current Situation**: Only 28 of 152 upper-tier local authorities in England have published climate adaptation plans. Those that exist vary enormously in quality, evidence base, and measurability. The CCC's 2025 adaptation progress report rated local adaptation as "insufficient" for the fifth consecutive year. Most local authority climate officers have 0.5-2 FTEs covering both mitigation and adaptation — adaptation is typically deprioritised in favour of emissions reduction targets and statutory mitigation duties.

**Specific Pain Points**:

| Stakeholder | Driver ID | Pain Point | Impact | Intensity |
|-------------|-----------|------------|--------|-----------|
| DEFRA Adaptation | SD-1 | CCC criticism of local adaptation gap | NAP3 commitment undelivered | CRITICAL |
| LGA | SD-2 | No practical tools, no new burdens funding | LAs unable to respond | HIGH |
| CCC Adaptation | SD-3 | Cannot assess local adaptation systematically | Statutory reporting gap | HIGH |
| LA climate officers | SD-4 | 6 months to produce a plan from scratch | Capacity overwhelmed | HIGH |

**Consequences of Inaction**:

- CCC continues to rate local adaptation as "insufficient" in annual reports
- Local climate damage costs escalate (GBP 1.2B annually, projected to double by 2050)
- Local authorities face climate-related service disruption without planned adaptation
- NAP3 commitment undelivered — Ministerial embarrassment at CCC progress assessment
- Adaptation gap widens as climate impacts accelerate

### A1.2 Strategic Alignment

- **NAP3**: "Provide tools and evidence to support local adaptation planning"
- **CCRA3**: Implementation of local adaptation recommendations
- **DEFRA 25 Year Environment Plan**: Resilient communities and infrastructure
- **Architecture Principles**: Accessibility (P16), User-Centred Design (adapted for LAs)

### A1.3 Why Now?

- NAP3 mid-term review 2027 — tool must be live to demonstrate progress
- Climate Risk Assessment Platform (Project 002) provides data foundation
- CCC CCRA4 scoping begins 2028 — tool data transforms evidence base
- Increasing climate impacts (2023/24 storms, 2025 heatwaves) create political urgency
