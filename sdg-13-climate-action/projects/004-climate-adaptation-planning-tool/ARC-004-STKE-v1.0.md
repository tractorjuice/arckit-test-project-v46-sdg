## Executive Summary

### Purpose

This document identifies key stakeholders for the Climate Adaptation Planning Tool, a digital service enabling local authorities to develop, manage, and monitor climate adaptation plans. The tool will provide evidence-based templates, risk-to-action mapping, and progress tracking to support the 152 upper-tier local authorities in England in planning for climate impacts including flooding, overheating, drought, and coastal erosion.

### Key Findings

The strongest alignment exists between DEFRA's policy ambition to improve local adaptation and the Local Government Association's recognition that most councils lack the tools and expertise to develop effective adaptation plans. The CCC Adaptation Committee's repeated criticism of the "adaptation gap" at local level provides political momentum. The primary tension is between DEFRA's preference for a standardised national approach and local authorities' insistence on flexibility to reflect local circumstances. A secondary tension exists between the desire for comprehensive planning tools and the severe capacity constraints facing local authority climate teams (typically 0.5-2 FTEs).

### Critical Success Factors

- Achieve adoption by at least 50% of upper-tier authorities within 18 months — tool must be usable by authorities with minimal climate expertise
- Integrate with the Climate Risk Assessment Platform (Project 002) to provide locally relevant risk evidence
- Deliver template adaptation plans that can be customised to local context without starting from scratch
- Maintain GDS accessibility standards — local authority users include part-time officers, elected members, and community partners
- Demonstrate alignment with the National Adaptation Programme (NAP3) objectives

### Stakeholder Alignment Score

**Overall Alignment**: MEDIUM

Strong consensus on the need for better local adaptation tools, but significant disagreement on the degree of standardisation vs local flexibility, the level of central government oversight, and the funding model for ongoing tool maintenance. Local authorities are supportive in principle but resistant to unfunded mandates.

## Stakeholder Drivers Analysis

### SD-1: DEFRA Adaptation Policy — Close the Local Adaptation Gap

**Stakeholder**: DEFRA Climate Adaptation Policy Team

**Driver Category**: STRATEGIC / COMPLIANCE

**Driver Statement**: Deliver a tool that demonstrably closes the local adaptation gap identified by the CCC, enabling DEFRA to show progress in the National Adaptation Programme (NAP3) assessment and respond to CCC criticism that government is not doing enough to support local adaptation.

**Context & Background**:
The CCC's Independent Assessment of UK Climate Risk (CCRA3) and subsequent progress reports have consistently identified local adaptation as a critical gap. The CCC's 2025 progress report rated local adaptation planning as "insufficient" with "very little evidence of coordinated, evidence-based local adaptation planning outside a handful of leading authorities." DEFRA's NAP3 commits to "providing tools and evidence to support local adaptation" — this tool is the primary deliverable against that commitment.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Tool adoption by a critical mass of local authorities (50%+ within 18 months)
- Demonstrable improvement in adaptation plan quality (CCC-assessed)
- Integration with Climate Risk Assessment Platform (Project 002) for evidence base
- NAP3 progress reporting dashboard showing tool adoption and plan completion rates

**Blockers**:

- Local authorities unwilling to adopt a centrally mandated tool
- Insufficient DEFRA funding for local authority adoption support
- Tool too complex for authorities with limited climate capacity
- CCC criticism of the tool's methodology or scope

**Related Stakeholders**: CCC Adaptation Committee (scrutiny), LGA (adoption), Local authority climate officers (users)

---

### SD-2: Local Government Association — Practical Support, Not Unfunded Mandates

**Stakeholder**: Local Government Association (LGA)

**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL / FINANCIAL

**Driver Statement**: Ensure the tool provides genuine practical support to local authorities without creating additional unfunded obligations, respects local democratic decision-making on adaptation priorities, and does not impose a one-size-fits-all framework that ignores local context.

**Context & Background**:
Local authorities are under severe financial pressure following years of austerity. Most councils do not have dedicated climate adaptation officers — the work falls to sustainability officers, emergency planners, or planning policy officers alongside other duties. The LGA supports the principle of better adaptation tools but is wary of central government initiatives that create new expectations without new funding. The LGA's "Climate Change — A Guide for Councillors" emphasises local flexibility and democratic oversight.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Free tool with no licensing costs for local authorities
- Minimal training requirements — usable by non-specialists within 2 hours
- Templates that can be customised to local priorities and circumstances
- No mandatory reporting requirements unless accompanied by new burdens funding
- Case studies from peer authorities demonstrating practical value

**Blockers**:

- Central government mandating tool use without funding
- Rigid templates that don't accommodate local circumstances
- Data entry burden disproportionate to local authority capacity
- Tool becoming a monitoring/reporting mechanism for central government oversight

**Related Stakeholders**: DEFRA Adaptation Policy (commissioner), Local authority climate officers (users), ADEPT (senior officer perspective), Elected members (democratic oversight)

---

### SD-3: CCC Adaptation Committee — Evidence-Based, Measurable Adaptation Progress

**Stakeholder**: CCC Adaptation Committee

**Driver Category**: COMPLIANCE / STRATEGIC

**Driver Statement**: Ensure the tool produces adaptation plans that are evidence-based, measurable, and enable the CCC to assess whether local adaptation is improving in quality and coverage — providing the data needed for the CCC's statutory reporting on adaptation progress.

**Context & Background**:
The CCC Adaptation Committee is responsible for advising on the UK Climate Change Risk Assessment (CCRA) and assessing progress on the National Adaptation Programme. The Committee has consistently identified the difficulty of assessing local adaptation progress due to lack of standardised data. A tool that produces structured, comparable adaptation plans would significantly improve the CCC's ability to report on local adaptation progress.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Structured output format enabling aggregation and comparison across authorities
- Risk-to-action mapping that demonstrates how plans address identified risks
- Progress tracking with measurable indicators
- Open data publication of anonymised plan summaries

**Blockers**:

- Tool producing free-text plans with no structured data
- No progress tracking or outcome measurement capability
- Adoption too low to provide meaningful national picture
- Plans not linked to evidence base (CCRA, UKCP18)

**Related Stakeholders**: DEFRA Adaptation Policy (tool commissioner), Local authority climate officers (plan producers)

---

### SD-4: Local Authority Climate Officers — Usable Tools That Save Time

**Stakeholder**: Local authority climate and sustainability officers

**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL / PERSONAL

**Driver Statement**: Access practical, time-saving tools that help produce credible adaptation plans quickly, provide evidence to support business cases for adaptation investment, and demonstrate professional competence to elected members and senior officers.

**Context & Background**:
Most local authority climate officers are managing adaptation alongside mitigation, sustainability, and often other environmental duties. They typically have 0.5-2 FTEs for climate work covering both mitigation and adaptation. Many are relatively junior in the organisation and need evidence and tools to make the case for adaptation investment to finance directors and elected members. A good tool would save them weeks of work that currently goes into producing adaptation plans from scratch or adapting generic guidance.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Pre-populated risk assessments from Project 002, reducing initial data gathering
- Template adaptation plans with sector-specific actions (housing, transport, health, biodiversity)
- Evidence summaries suitable for presenting to elected members (plain language, visual)
- Peer benchmarking — how does our authority compare to similar areas?
- Export formats suitable for committee reports and public consultation documents

**Blockers**:

- Tool requiring extensive data entry before providing value
- Complex interface requiring specialist training
- No integration with existing local authority systems
- Outputs not suitable for non-technical audiences (elected members, public)

**Related Stakeholders**: LGA (representative body), ADEPT (senior officers), Elected members (plan approval)

## Driver-to-Goal Mapping

### Goal G-1: Achieve 50% Local Authority Adoption Within 18 Months

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-1, SD-2, SD-4

**Goal Owner**: Service Owner

**Goal Statement**: Achieve registration and initial plan creation by at least 76 upper-tier authorities (50% of 152) within 18 months of public launch, through a free, low-friction onboarding process.

**Success Metrics**:

- **Primary Metric**: Number of authorities with active accounts and initiated plans
- **Secondary Metrics**:
  - Time from registration to first plan section completed (target: under 2 hours)
  - User satisfaction score 4/5+ in post-onboarding survey

---

### Goal G-2: Enable CCC-Assessable Adaptation Plans

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-3, SD-1

**Goal Owner**: DEFRA Adaptation Policy Lead

**Goal Statement**: Produce structured adaptation plan outputs that enable CCC to assess local adaptation progress at a national level, with standardised risk-to-action mapping and measurable progress indicators.

**Success Metrics**:

- **Primary Metric**: CCC able to include tool-generated data in 2028 CCRA4 preparatory work
- **Secondary Metrics**:
  - Standardised risk-action mappings covering 8+ CCRA risk areas
  - Progress indicators defined for 80%+ of plan actions

---

### Goal G-3: Reduce Adaptation Plan Development Time by 60%

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-4, SD-2

**Goal Owner**: Product Manager

**Goal Statement**: Reduce the average time for a local authority to produce a credible adaptation plan from approximately 6 months (current manual process) to under 10 weeks using the tool.

**Success Metrics**:

- **Primary Metric**: Average time from plan initiation to committee-ready draft under 10 weeks
- **Secondary Metrics**:
  - 80% of users report time savings vs previous approach
  - Plans rated as "evidence-based" by independent review

### Conflict Analysis

**Competing Drivers**:

- **Conflict 1**: DEFRA wants standardised, comparable outputs for national assessment (SD-1) vs LGA insists on local flexibility and no unfunded mandates (SD-2)
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Structured templates with mandatory core sections (for comparability) and optional local sections (for flexibility). Tool free to use, no mandatory reporting requirements.

- **Conflict 2**: CCC wants measurable, structured data (SD-3) vs climate officers need outputs suitable for elected members and public (SD-4)
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Dual output — structured data for CCC aggregation plus formatted committee reports and public-facing summaries.
