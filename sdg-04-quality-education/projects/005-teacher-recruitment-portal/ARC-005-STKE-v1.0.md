## Executive Summary

### Purpose

This document identifies key stakeholders for the Teacher Recruitment Portal (TRP), their underlying drivers, and how these translate into goals and measurable outcomes. The TRP will provide a unified digital service for teacher training recruitment, replacing the fragmented landscape of UCAS Teacher Training, Get Into Teaching, and individual provider application systems.

### Key Findings

England faces a persistent teacher recruitment crisis, with secondary subject recruitment targets missed for 11 consecutive years in priority subjects (Physics, Maths, MFL, Computing). The current recruitment journey is fragmented — candidates must navigate Get Into Teaching for information, UCAS Teacher Training or DfE Apply for applications, and individual provider websites for course details. This fragmentation disproportionately deters career changers (who have less time to research) and candidates from underrepresented backgrounds (who lack informal networks to navigate the system). The strongest alignment exists around simplifying the application journey and improving data-driven marketing to attract candidates into shortage subjects.

### Critical Success Factors

- Increase applications for secondary shortage subjects by 25% within 2 recruitment cycles
- Achieve 90% of ITT applications submitted through the unified portal within 18 months
- Reduce application abandonment rate from 35% to below 15%
- Improve diversity of applicant pool (BAME, male primary, career changers)
- Integrate with DBS checking service to reduce time from offer to classroom

### Stakeholder Alignment Score

**Overall Alignment**: MEDIUM-HIGH

Strong consensus on the need for a better recruitment experience. Key tension between DfE (centralised portal with allocation control) and ITT providers (who want to maintain direct relationships with candidates and fear losing recruitment autonomy).

## Stakeholder Drivers Analysis

### SD-1: Minister for Schools — Solve the Teacher Recruitment Crisis

**Stakeholder**: Minister for Schools

**Driver Category**: POLITICAL / STRATEGIC

**Driver Statement**: Resolve the persistent teacher recruitment crisis in secondary shortage subjects (Physics, Maths, MFL, Computing, Chemistry, DT) by making the route into teaching simpler, more attractive, and more accessible to career changers and underrepresented groups.

**Context & Background**: Secondary ITT recruitment targets have been missed for 11 consecutive years in Physics (38% of target in 2024/25), Maths (68%), MFL (52%), and Computing (44%). Teacher vacancies are at their highest level in a decade. The Minister faces regular parliamentary questions and media criticism about classroom shortages affecting pupil outcomes.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

---

### SD-2: ITT Providers — Maintain Recruitment Autonomy and Provider Identity

**Stakeholder**: ITT Providers (Universities, SCITTs, School Direct lead schools)

**Driver Category**: STRATEGIC / RISK

**Driver Statement**: Ensure that any centralised recruitment portal enhances rather than undermines provider recruitment capability, maintaining provider identity, course differentiation, and direct candidate relationships throughout the application process.

**Context & Background**: ITT providers have invested significantly in recruitment marketing, open events, and candidate nurturing. They fear a centralised portal will commoditise their courses, reduce their visibility, and give DfE control over candidate allocation. Smaller providers (SCITTs, School Direct) are particularly vulnerable as they lack the marketing budgets of large universities.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

---

### SD-3: Career Changers — Clear, Simple Entry Route

**Stakeholder**: Prospective Teachers (Career Changers, typically 25-45)

**Driver Category**: CUSTOMER / PERSONAL

**Driver Statement**: Understand clearly and quickly whether teaching is right for them, what qualifications and experience they need, what financial support is available, and how to apply — without navigating multiple websites, acronyms, and confusing route names (PGCE, QTS, SCITT, School Direct, Teach First).

**Context & Background**: Career changers represent the largest untapped pool for shortage subjects — a Physics graduate working in industry could address the Physics teacher shortage but faces a bewildering array of training routes, confusing eligibility criteria, and a 6-month application process. Research shows career changers typically make a decision within 3 weeks of first considering teaching — if the journey is too complex, they abandon it.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

---

### SD-4: UCAS — Partnership and Transition

**Stakeholder**: UCAS

**Driver Category**: STRATEGIC / FINANCIAL

**Driver Statement**: Manage the transition from UCAS Teacher Training to DfE's new portal in a way that preserves UCAS's reputation, minimises disruption to providers and candidates, and allows UCAS to retain a meaningful role in teacher recruitment data and matching.

**Context & Background**: UCAS has operated the Teacher Training application service for over a decade but DfE has been developing its own Apply for Teacher Training (DfE Apply) service. The relationship is complex — UCAS wants to retain revenue and market position, while DfE wants control over the end-to-end candidate journey. The transition must be managed carefully to avoid a service gap.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

---

### SD-5: School Leadership — Access to Qualified Teachers in Shortage Areas

**Stakeholder**: Headteachers and MAT CEOs

**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL / RISK

**Driver Statement**: Ensure the recruitment portal delivers a pipeline of qualified teachers in shortage subjects and hard-to-recruit areas (coastal, rural, areas of high deprivation), reducing the reliance on expensive agency supply teachers.

**Context & Background**: Schools spend approximately GBP 1.3B annually on supply teachers, much of which could be avoided with better permanent recruitment. MAT CEOs want visibility of the teacher pipeline for their geographic area and subject specialisms. Schools in disadvantaged areas struggle most — the schools with the greatest need have the hardest time recruiting.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

---

### SD-6: DBS and TRA — Streamlined Pre-Employment Checks

**Stakeholder**: Disclosure and Barring Service and Teaching Regulation Agency

**Driver Category**: COMPLIANCE / OPERATIONAL

**Driver Statement**: Integrate DBS enhanced disclosure and TRA prohibition checks into the recruitment workflow to reduce the time from conditional offer to classroom start, while maintaining the rigour of safeguarding checks.

**Driver Intensity**: MEDIUM

## Conflict Analysis

- **Conflict 1**: DfE (SD-1) wants a centralised portal with unified candidate journey, but ITT providers (SD-2) want to maintain direct recruitment relationships and course differentiation
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Compromise — unified application portal but with rich provider profiles, course comparison features, and provider-branded communication channels within the platform. Providers retain ability to host open events and market directly, with portal as the application submission channel.

- **Conflict 2**: UCAS (SD-4) wants to retain teacher training application revenue, but DfE wants full ownership of the candidate journey
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Phase — negotiate a transition period with UCAS; maintain data sharing for analytics; explore UCAS role in international teacher recruitment (where DfE has no existing capability).

- **Conflict 3**: Career changers (SD-3) want a simplified route, but the system has genuinely complex routes (QTS, PGCE, School Direct salaried/unsalaried, SCITT, Teach First) that cannot be oversimplified without misleading candidates
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Innovate — build a "route finder" tool that asks the candidate about their circumstances (degree subject, work experience, financial needs) and recommends suitable routes with plain-language explanations. Complexity is managed by the system, not imposed on the user.
