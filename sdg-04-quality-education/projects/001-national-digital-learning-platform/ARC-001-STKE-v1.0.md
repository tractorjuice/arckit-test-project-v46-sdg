## Executive Summary

### Purpose

This document identifies key stakeholders for the National Digital Learning Platform (NDLP), their underlying drivers, how these drivers manifest into goals, and the measurable outcomes that will satisfy those goals. This analysis ensures stakeholder alignment and provides traceability from individual concerns to programme success metrics.

### Key Findings

The NDLP programme reveals a fundamental tension between Ministerial ambition for a unified national platform and the deeply federated reality of English education — 22,000+ schools with significant autonomy, diverse MIS systems, and strong headteacher resistance to top-down mandates. The strongest alignment exists around reducing the digital divide and improving access to quality learning resources, which satisfies political, educational, and equity drivers simultaneously. The most significant conflict is between the ambition for a comprehensive platform and the commercial interests of existing EdTech providers who view government intervention as market distortion.

### Critical Success Factors

- Achieve voluntary adoption by at least 40% of schools within 18 months through demonstrable value, not mandate
- Maintain curriculum neutrality — the platform must support diverse pedagogical approaches without imposing a single teaching methodology
- Integrate seamlessly with major school MIS systems (SIMS, Bromcom, Arbor) to avoid duplicate data entry
- Demonstrate measurable narrowing of the attainment gap for pupil premium eligible students using the platform
- Pass GDS service assessment at Beta to maintain CDDO confidence and unlock Phase 2 funding

### Stakeholder Alignment Score

**Overall Alignment**: MEDIUM

Strong consensus on the need to address digital inequality in education, but significant tensions between centralised platform ambition (DfE), school autonomy (headteachers/MAT CEOs), commercial market concerns (EdTech sector), and fiscal caution (HM Treasury). Teaching unions add complexity through workload concerns and data privacy advocacy.

## Stakeholder Drivers Analysis

### SD-1: Secretary of State — Closing the Digital Divide in Education

**Stakeholder**: Secretary of State for Education

**Driver Category**: POLITICAL / STRATEGIC

**Driver Statement**: Deliver a flagship national digital learning platform that demonstrably narrows the attainment gap between disadvantaged and non-disadvantaged pupils, providing a visible response to post-pandemic learning loss and digital inequality.

**Context & Background**:
The COVID-19 pandemic exposed severe digital inequality in English education. An estimated 1.78 million children lacked adequate device access or connectivity during lockdowns. The attainment gap widened by approximately 0.5 months for disadvantaged pupils at Key Stage 2. The Secretary of State faces ongoing parliamentary scrutiny on catch-up funding effectiveness and needs a tangible, scalable digital intervention to demonstrate progress against SDG 4.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Cross-party support for digital inclusion in education
- Existing investment in devices through the Get Help with Technology programme
- Oak National Academy providing proof of concept for free curriculum resources

**Blockers**:

- Treasury reluctance to fund another large DfE digital programme after historical overruns
- EdTech industry lobbying against government market intervention
- School autonomy culture resistant to centralised platforms

**Related Stakeholders**: HM Treasury, MAT CEOs, Headteachers, Pupil Premium Policy Team

---

### SD-2: MAT CEOs — Operational Efficiency and Consistent Quality

**Stakeholder**: Multi-Academy Trust Chief Executive Officers

**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL / STRATEGIC

**Driver Statement**: Achieve consistent quality of digital learning resources across all trust schools while reducing the cost and administrative burden of procuring, evaluating, and managing multiple EdTech platforms independently.

**Context & Background**:
Large MATs (e.g., Harris Federation, Ark Schools, United Learning) operate 30-80 schools and spend GBP 500K-2M annually on EdTech licensing. Each school may use different platforms, making quality assurance, staff training, and data aggregation difficult. MAT CEOs want a single platform that provides trust-wide visibility of pupil progress while respecting individual school pedagogical approaches.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- MATs have central procurement and IT teams that can drive adoption at scale
- Existing frustration with fragmented EdTech market
- Trust-wide data and analytics a key strategic priority

**Blockers**:

- Reluctance to abandon existing platforms with sunk costs and training investment
- Concerns about government control over curriculum delivery
- Need for platform to support MAT-specific branding and customisation

**Related Stakeholders**: Headteachers, Teachers, EdTech Industry, DfE Finance Director

---

### SD-3: Headteachers — Teacher Workload and Pedagogical Freedom

**Stakeholder**: Headteachers and Principals

**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL / PERSONAL

**Driver Statement**: Reduce teacher workload associated with finding, adapting, and creating digital learning resources while preserving school-level autonomy over curriculum delivery and pedagogical approach.

**Context & Background**:
The DfE Teacher Workload Survey consistently identifies resource preparation as a significant contributor to unsustainable working hours. Teachers spend an estimated 3-5 hours per week finding and adapting resources. However, headteachers are protective of their school's distinctive curriculum offer and will resist any platform perceived as imposing a "one-size-fits-all" pedagogy. The NDLP must save time without constraining professional judgement.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Strong teacher demand for quality, ready-to-use resources
- Oak National Academy's success demonstrates appetite for free, high-quality curriculum resources
- DfE commitment to reducing teacher workload (Workload Reduction Toolkit)

**Blockers**:

- Distrust of government imposing curriculum through technology
- Concerns about platform quality compared to established commercial alternatives
- Fear of accountability metrics being derived from platform usage data

**Related Stakeholders**: Teachers, Teaching Unions, Curriculum & Standards Directorate, Ofsted

---

### SD-4: Parents and Carers — Supporting Learning at Home

**Stakeholder**: Parents and Carers

**Driver Category**: CUSTOMER / PERSONAL

**Driver Statement**: Access simple, trustworthy tools that help them support their child's learning at home, understand their child's progress, and engage meaningfully with their child's education — regardless of their own educational background or digital confidence.

**Context & Background**:
Parental engagement is the single strongest predictor of educational achievement. However, many parents — particularly those from disadvantaged backgrounds — feel excluded from digital learning platforms that assume high digital literacy, reliable broadband, and familiarity with educational terminology. The NDLP must be as accessible to a parent who left school at 16 with no qualifications as to a university-educated professional.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Strong parental desire to support children's education (EEF research evidence)
- Increasing smartphone ownership across all socioeconomic groups
- GOV.UK design patterns proven effective for low-literacy audiences

**Blockers**:

- Digital exclusion — estimated 7% of UK households have no internet access
- Language barriers for parents with English as an additional language
- Privacy concerns about children's data being visible to government

**Related Stakeholders**: Pupils/Students, SEND Sector, ICO, Teaching Unions

---

### SD-5: Ofsted — Evidence-Informed Quality of Education

**Stakeholder**: Ofsted

**Driver Category**: STRATEGIC / COMPLIANCE

**Driver Statement**: Ensure that any national learning platform supports and does not undermine the quality of education framework, providing inspectors with evidence of curriculum intent, implementation, and impact without creating perverse incentives around platform usage metrics.

**Context & Background**:
Ofsted's Education Inspection Framework (EIF) assesses quality of education based on curriculum intent, implementation, and impact. Ofsted is concerned that a national platform could create perverse incentives — schools optimising for platform engagement metrics rather than genuine learning outcomes. Equally, Ofsted sees potential value in a platform that helps schools access high-quality curriculum resources aligned with a knowledge-rich curriculum approach.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Ofsted's support for a knowledge-rich curriculum aligns with structured resource provision
- Platform data could provide useful contextual information for inspections (if not misused)
- Potential to reduce variability in curriculum quality across schools

**Blockers**:

- Risk of platform becoming a compliance tool rather than a pedagogical aid
- Ofsted cannot be seen to endorse specific resources or platforms
- Concern about data being used for accountability rather than improvement

**Related Stakeholders**: Headteachers, Teachers, Curriculum & Standards Directorate, Teaching Unions

---

### SD-6: Teaching Unions — Workload, Privacy, and Professional Autonomy

**Stakeholder**: Teaching Unions (NEU, NASUWT, ASCL, NAHT)

**Driver Category**: COMPLIANCE / PERSONAL

**Driver Statement**: Ensure that the platform genuinely reduces teacher workload, does not create surveillance mechanisms for monitoring teacher performance, and respects teachers' professional autonomy in curriculum delivery.

**Context & Background**:
Teaching unions are instinctively cautious about government-mandated digital platforms. Historical concerns include: platforms used to monitor teacher "engagement" as a proxy for performance management; increased workload from mandatory platform use adding to existing demands; and erosion of professional autonomy through prescriptive resource provision. The unions will support the NDLP only if it demonstrably reduces workload and does not create new accountability mechanisms.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Genuine teacher frustration with resource preparation workload
- Union willingness to engage constructively if workload reduction is evidenced
- Platform can be positioned as supporting, not replacing, professional judgement

**Blockers**:

- Any suggestion of mandatory adoption will trigger union opposition
- Usage analytics must not be accessible to school leaders for performance management
- Platform must not replace the need for qualified teachers

**Related Stakeholders**: Headteachers, Teachers, Secretary of State, DfE SIRO

---

### SD-7: EdTech Industry — Market Impact and Partnership Opportunities

**Stakeholder**: EdTech Industry (Oak National Academy, Seneca Learning, Sparx Maths, Kognity, etc.)

**Driver Category**: FINANCIAL / STRATEGIC

**Driver Statement**: Protect existing market position and revenue while exploring partnership opportunities with the platform, ensuring that government intervention does not distort the EdTech market or crowd out private innovation.

**Context & Background**:
The UK EdTech market is worth approximately GBP 3.4 billion. Commercial providers have invested heavily in curriculum-aligned content, adaptive learning algorithms, and school relationships. A free government platform could undermine their business models. However, some providers see partnership opportunities — providing premium content or specialist tools through a government marketplace.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Partnership model (government marketplace for EdTech) could expand market reach
- Platform could drive digital adoption, increasing overall market size
- Quality assurance framework could help legitimate providers differentiate from poor-quality competitors

**Blockers**:

- Free government platform directly competes with commercial offerings
- Procurement rules may disadvantage smaller innovative providers
- Data portability requirements could undermine vendor lock-in strategies

**Related Stakeholders**: HM Treasury, Secretary of State, MAT CEOs, Headteachers

---

### SD-8: ICO — Children's Data Protection and Age Appropriate Design

**Stakeholder**: Information Commissioner's Office

**Driver Category**: COMPLIANCE / RISK

**Driver Statement**: Ensure that the platform fully complies with the Age Appropriate Design Code, UK GDPR, and the Data Protection Act 2018, serving as a model for responsible processing of children's data at scale.

**Context & Background**:
The ICO has signalled that education technology is a priority enforcement area. The Age Appropriate Design Code applies to information society services likely to be accessed by children, which the NDLP unambiguously is. The ICO expects the platform to demonstrate best practice in data minimisation, age-appropriate transparency, high privacy defaults, and restrictions on profiling or nudge techniques. Given the scale (potentially millions of children), any non-compliance would attract significant regulatory attention.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Clear regulatory framework (AADC) provides specific, actionable requirements
- DfE's existing relationship with ICO through the National Pupil Database
- Opportunity for the platform to be a reference implementation of AADC compliance

**Blockers**:

- Tension between personalised learning (requiring data processing) and data minimisation
- Parental consent mechanisms at scale are complex and costly
- Third-party EdTech content providers may not meet AADC standards

**Related Stakeholders**: DfE SIRO, Parents, Pupils, SEND Sector

## Driver-to-Goal Mapping

### Goal G-1: Achieve 40% School Adoption Within 18 Months

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-1, SD-2, SD-3

**Goal Owner**: SRO, NDLP Programme

**Goal Statement**: Achieve voluntary adoption of the National Digital Learning Platform by at least 40% of state-funded schools in England (approximately 8,800 schools) within 18 months of public beta launch.

**Why This Matters**: Without significant voluntary adoption, the platform cannot deliver the Secretary of State's digital divide objectives (SD-1), MATs cannot achieve trust-wide consistency (SD-2), and the investment cannot be justified to Treasury. Mandatory adoption is politically untenable, so the platform must be so useful that schools choose to adopt it.

**Success Metrics**:

- **Primary Metric**: Number of schools with at least 10 active teacher accounts
- **Secondary Metrics**:
  - Monthly active teacher users
  - Geographic spread of adoption (ensuring rural and disadvantaged areas are represented)
  - MAT-level adoption rate

**Baseline**: 0 schools (new platform)
**Target**: 8,800 schools with active usage
**Measurement Method**: Platform analytics, DfE Sign-in authentication logs

**Dependencies**:

- MIS integration with SIMS, Bromcom, Arbor must be complete before launch
- DfE Sign-in SSO must be reliable and performant
- Sufficient curriculum content must be available at launch (minimum KS1-KS4 core subjects)

**Risks to Achievement**:

- Headteacher resistance to "yet another government platform"
- MIS integration delays reducing platform utility
- Commercial EdTech providers offering competitive counter-promotions

---

### Goal G-2: Narrow the Disadvantaged Attainment Gap by 0.3 Months Within 2 Years

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-1, SD-4

**Goal Owner**: DfE Pupil Premium Policy Team

**Goal Statement**: Demonstrate a measurable narrowing of the attainment gap between pupil premium eligible students and their peers by 0.3 months of progress at Key Stage 2 within 2 years of platform launch, as measured by national assessment data.

**Why This Matters**: This goal directly addresses the Secretary of State's core political driver and SDG 4's equity mandate. Without measurable impact on disadvantage, the platform risks being dismissed as a technology project rather than an education intervention.

**Success Metrics**:

- **Primary Metric**: Attainment gap (months of progress) between FSM-eligible and non-FSM pupils at KS2
- **Secondary Metrics**:
  - Platform usage rates disaggregated by FSM eligibility
  - Teacher-reported impact on learning for disadvantaged pupils
  - Homework completion rates for disadvantaged pupils using the platform

**Baseline**: Current attainment gap (approximately 18 months at KS2)
**Target**: Reduce by 0.3 months to approximately 17.7 months
**Measurement Method**: National Pupil Database analysis, platform usage analytics linked to NPD data

---

### Goal G-3: Reduce Teacher Resource Preparation Time by 2 Hours per Week

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-3, SD-6

**Goal Owner**: NDLP Service Owner

**Goal Statement**: Reduce the average time teachers spend finding, evaluating, and adapting digital learning resources from 4 hours per week to 2 hours per week within 12 months of adoption.

**Why This Matters**: Teacher workload is the primary adoption driver and the primary concern of teaching unions. If the platform does not measurably save time, teachers will abandon it and unions will campaign against it.

**Success Metrics**:

- **Primary Metric**: Self-reported weekly hours spent on resource preparation (teacher survey)
- **Secondary Metrics**:
  - Platform resource download and reuse rates
  - Teacher satisfaction scores
  - Reduction in commercial EdTech subscription spend per school

**Baseline**: 4 hours/week (DfE Teacher Workload Survey)
**Target**: 2 hours/week
**Measurement Method**: Annual teacher workload survey, platform usage analytics

---

### Goal G-4: Achieve Full AADC and WCAG 2.2 AA Compliance at Launch

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-8, SD-4

**Goal Owner**: DfE CDO

**Goal Statement**: Achieve full compliance with the ICO Age Appropriate Design Code (all 15 standards) and WCAG 2.2 Level AA for all user-facing components at public beta launch, verified by independent audit.

**Why This Matters**: Non-compliance with the AADC for a service processing millions of children's data would trigger ICO enforcement action and severely damage the platform's credibility. Accessibility compliance is both a legal requirement and essential for the inclusive education mission.

**Success Metrics**:

- **Primary Metric**: Independent AADC compliance audit result (pass/fail per standard)
- **Secondary Metrics**:
  - WCAG 2.2 AA automated test pass rate
  - User testing with SEND pupils satisfaction scores
  - Accessibility statement published and accurate

**Baseline**: N/A (new platform)
**Target**: 15/15 AADC standards met; 100% WCAG 2.2 AA conformance
**Measurement Method**: Independent audit by ICO-approved assessor; GDS accessibility assessment

---

### Goal G-5: Integrate with Top 3 MIS Providers Within 6 Months

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-2, SD-3

**Goal Owner**: NDLP Technical Lead

**Goal Statement**: Deliver working bidirectional integration with SIMS (Capita), Bromcom, and Arbor (covering approximately 85% of schools) within 6 months of development start, enabling single sign-on, class list synchronisation, and pupil data exchange without manual data entry.

**Why This Matters**: Without MIS integration, teachers must manually re-enter class lists and pupil data, creating exactly the workload burden the platform aims to eliminate. MIS integration is the single biggest determinant of teacher adoption.

**Success Metrics**:

- **Primary Metric**: Percentage of schools where MIS integration is functional
- **Secondary Metrics**:
  - Time to onboard a new school (target: under 30 minutes)
  - Data synchronisation accuracy (target: 99.9%)
  - Teacher-reported ease of setup (NPS)

**Baseline**: 0% (new integrations)
**Target**: 85% of schools covered by MIS integration
**Measurement Method**: Integration testing, school onboarding metrics

### Conflict Analysis

**Competing Drivers**:

- **Conflict 1**: Secretary of State (SD-1) wants rapid, high-visibility platform launch, but ICO (SD-8) requires thorough AADC compliance assessment that takes time
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Phase — launch with core AADC-compliant features; add personalisation features only after full AADC audit. Accept slower personalisation roadmap to maintain compliance.

- **Conflict 2**: MAT CEOs (SD-2) want trust-wide data dashboards and analytics, but Teaching Unions (SD-6) oppose any mechanism that could be used for teacher performance monitoring
