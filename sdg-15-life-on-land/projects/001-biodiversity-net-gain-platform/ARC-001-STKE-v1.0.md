## Executive Summary

### Purpose

This document identifies key stakeholders for the Biodiversity Net Gain Platform, their underlying drivers (motivations, concerns, pressures), how these drivers manifest into goals, and the measurable outcomes that will satisfy those goals. This analysis ensures stakeholder alignment and provides traceability from individual concerns to programme success metrics.

### Key Findings

The BNG Platform faces a fundamental tension between the urgency of delivering a functioning credit marketplace (developers cannot commence building without demonstrating BNG compliance) and the scientific rigour required to ensure biodiversity credits represent genuine ecological value. The strongest alignment exists around establishing a trusted, transparent credit registry — developers need certainty to progress planning applications, habitat banks need market confidence to invest in habitat creation, and Natural England needs assurance that the 10% minimum net gain is genuinely delivered. The most significant conflict is between the development industry's desire for speed and flexibility in discharging BNG obligations and conservation organisations' insistence on robust ecological assessment and 30-year management accountability.

### Critical Success Factors

- Deliver a functioning BNG credit marketplace before the small sites mandate takes effect, to avoid a bottleneck in planning decisions nationally
- Ensure Biodiversity Metric 4.0 calculations are transparent, auditable, and independently verifiable to maintain ecological credibility
- Establish a trusted credit registry that prevents double-counting, fraud, and speculative hoarding of biodiversity credits
- Enable Local Planning Authorities to verify BNG compliance efficiently within existing planning determination timescales
- Maintain 30-year habitat management accountability through enforceable obligations and monitoring mechanisms

### Stakeholder Alignment Score

**Overall Alignment**: MEDIUM

Strong alignment on the need for a digital BNG platform and credit registry, but significant tensions between development speed (housing developers, LPAs), ecological rigour (Natural England, conservation charities), market liquidity (habitat banks, landowners), and regulatory oversight (DEFRA policy). The 30-year management obligation creates particular complexity around long-term accountability and financial guarantees.

## Stakeholder Drivers Analysis

### SD-1: Secretary of State (DEFRA) — Demonstrating Environmental Leadership

**Stakeholder**: Secretary of State for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs

**Driver Category**: POLITICAL / STRATEGIC

**Driver Statement**: Deliver a world-leading biodiversity credit marketplace that demonstrates the UK's commitment to halting biodiversity decline, enabling positive responses to international scrutiny at CBD COP and domestically in Parliament, while ensuring the BNG mandate does not become a barrier to housebuilding targets.

**Context & Background**:
The Environment Act 2021 made mandatory BNG a landmark UK environmental policy — the first country to require measurable biodiversity improvement as a condition of planning permission. The Secretary of State faces dual pressure: environmentalists expect rigorous implementation that genuinely reverses biodiversity decline, while the housing sector and Treasury expect BNG to operate efficiently without delaying the government's housebuilding targets. The 30by30 commitment adds international visibility via UN reporting.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Rapid delivery of a functioning credit marketplace to prevent planning bottlenecks
- Positive case studies of habitat creation funded through BNG credits
- Transparent metrics showing measurable biodiversity gains at national scale
- Efficient developer journey that does not materially delay planning timescales

**Blockers**:

- Planning bottlenecks caused by BNG compliance delays (political damage from housing targets)
- Ecological criticism that credits represent "greenwash" rather than genuine biodiversity gain
- Market failures — credit prices too high for SME developers or too low to incentivise habitat creation
- System outages or errors that undermine confidence in the digital platform

**Related Stakeholders**: Permanent Secretary (accountability), Natural England (scientific credibility), Housing developers (compliance burden), Conservation charities (ecological rigour)

---

### SD-2: Natural England Chief Scientist — Ecological Credibility of the Metric

**Stakeholder**: Natural England Chief Scientist

**Driver Category**: SCIENTIFIC / COMPLIANCE

**Driver Statement**: Ensure the Biodiversity Metric 4.0 calculations within the digital platform are scientifically robust, transparent, and produce ecologically meaningful results — protecting Natural England's scientific reputation and the integrity of the BNG policy.

**Context & Background**:
Natural England developed the Biodiversity Metric 4.0 as the standard tool for measuring biodiversity losses and gains from development. The metric involves complex multi-factor calculations (habitat distinctiveness, condition, strategic significance, temporal multipliers, spatial risk multipliers). Errors or oversimplifications in the digital implementation would undermine the scientific basis of the entire BNG policy. Previous digital implementations of ecological tools have attracted criticism for "black box" calculations that ecologists cannot verify.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Transparent, step-by-step calculation display showing all inputs, weightings, and intermediate results
- Independent verification capability — ecologists can replicate calculations manually
- Version control of metric parameters with clear change management process
- Integration with Natural England's habitat classification and condition assessment guidance

**Blockers**:

- Pressure to simplify the metric for developer convenience at the expense of ecological accuracy
- Inability to update metric parameters without major system changes (metric versions evolve)
- Insufficient ecological expertise in the development team leading to implementation errors
- Lack of validation against field ecologist assessments

**Related Stakeholders**: BNG Policy Lead (policy intent), Habitat banks (credit valuation), RSPB (scientific scrutiny), Ecologists (professional credibility)

---

### SD-3: Local Planning Authorities — Efficient BNG Compliance Verification

**Stakeholder**: Local Planning Authorities (collective — 333 LPAs in England)

**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL / COMPLIANCE

**Driver Statement**: Enable planning officers to verify BNG compliance quickly and confidently within existing planning determination timescales, without requiring specialist ecological expertise that most LPAs lack, and without creating an unfunded administrative burden.

**Context & Background**:
LPAs are responsible for enforcing BNG conditions as part of planning permission, but most lack in-house ecologists and have severely constrained budgets. The existing planning determination target is 8 weeks for minor applications and 13 weeks for major applications. Any BNG verification process that materially extends these timescales or requires significant additional resource will face strong resistance. Many LPAs are already struggling with planning officer recruitment and retention.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Automated BNG compliance checks that reduce manual ecological assessment
- Clear, standardised BNG condition templates that planning officers can apply without ecological expertise
- Integration with existing planning application management systems (e.g., Uniform, Idox)
- Training and guidance materials accessible to non-specialist planning officers

**Blockers**:

- Unfunded mandate — BNG enforcement costs borne by already-stretched LPA budgets
- Complexity of Biodiversity Metric 4.0 requiring specialist interpretation
- Lack of integration with LPA planning systems requiring double-keying of data
- Insufficient capacity to monitor 30-year habitat management obligations

**Related Stakeholders**: Planning Inspectorate (appeals), Housing developers (applicants), DEFRA Policy (mandate implementation), DLUHC (local government funding)

---

### SD-4: Housing Developers — Predictable, Cost-Effective BNG Compliance

**Stakeholder**: Housing developers (via Home Builders Federation)

**Driver Category**: FINANCIAL / OPERATIONAL

**Driver Statement**: Achieve BNG compliance through a clear, predictable, and cost-effective process that does not materially delay planning permissions or add disproportionate costs to housing development, with a functioning credit market providing a reliable fallback when on-site delivery is not feasible.

**Context & Background**:
The development industry accepted the principle of BNG but is concerned about implementation practicalities. On-site biodiversity delivery is preferred (lower cost, more control) but not always feasible, particularly on constrained urban sites. The statutory credit market (DEFRA credits at deliberately high prices to incentivise on-site delivery) and the emerging private credit market (habitat banks) provide alternatives. Developers need price certainty and clear timescales for credit availability to factor BNG costs into land acquisition decisions and project viability assessments.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Transparent credit pricing and availability information before land acquisition decisions
- Streamlined digital application process integrated with planning submission
- Clear guidance on acceptable on-site, off-site, and statutory credit approaches
- Rapid credit allocation and confirmation to avoid planning delays

**Blockers**:

- Volatile or opaque credit pricing making project viability uncertain
- Platform downtime or processing delays blocking planning determination
- Inconsistent LPA interpretation of BNG requirements creating postcode lottery
- Complex or duplicative data submission requirements

**Related Stakeholders**: LPAs (planning determination), Habitat banks (credit supply), Natural England (metric rules), Landowners (off-site delivery)

---

### SD-5: Habitat Banks — Viable Market for Biodiversity Credits

**Stakeholder**: Habitat banks and biodiversity credit providers

**Driver Category**: FINANCIAL / STRATEGIC

**Driver Statement**: Establish a transparent, liquid credit marketplace that provides confidence to invest significant upfront capital in habitat creation, with clear rules on credit valuation, registration, and 30-year management obligations that enable viable business models.

**Context & Background**:
Habitat banking is a nascent industry in England. Providers must invest significant capital upfront to acquire land, create habitats, and manage them for 30 years before receiving payment through credit sales. They need confidence in market demand, credit pricing, and regulatory stability to secure investment. The platform's credit registry is the foundation of their business model — it determines when credits can be sold, to whom, and at what price point.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Transparent credit registry showing supply, demand, and pricing signals
- Clear rules on credit creation, validation, and trading
- Efficient credit registration and verification process
- Market data and analytics to support investment decisions
- Long-term policy stability and regulatory certainty

**Blockers**:

- Uncertain or frequently changing metric rules affecting credit valuations
- Slow or opaque credit validation process delaying sales
- Competition from DEFRA statutory credits at prices below private market viability
- Lack of financial mechanisms for 30-year management obligations (bonds, insurance)

**Related Stakeholders**: DEFRA Policy (market design), Natural England (credit validation), Developers (credit purchasers), Landowners (land supply), Financial institutions (investment)

---

### SD-6: RSPB and Conservation Charities — Genuine Biodiversity Outcomes

**Stakeholder**: RSPB, The Wildlife Trusts, and broader conservation sector

**Driver Category**: ENVIRONMENTAL / COMPLIANCE

**Driver Statement**: Ensure the BNG Platform delivers genuine, measurable, and lasting biodiversity gains — not a tokenistic "greenwash" exercise that allows developers to discharge obligations through poor-quality habitat creation or creative metric manipulation.

**Context & Background**:
Conservation organisations supported the Environment Act 2021 BNG provisions but have consistently warned against implementation weaknesses. Key concerns include: metric manipulation (gaming habitat condition assessments to inflate baseline scores), poor-quality off-site delivery (low-value habitat creation distant from development impact), inadequate 30-year monitoring and enforcement, and cumulative loss of irreplaceable habitats not captured by the metric. These organisations have significant public platforms and can rapidly mobilise criticism if implementation falls short.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Robust quality assurance of habitat condition assessments with independent verification
- Transparent public register of BNG obligations, credits, and outcomes
- Strong monitoring and enforcement mechanisms for 30-year management plans
- Protection of irreplaceable habitats beyond the metric calculation
- Regular public reporting on aggregate biodiversity outcomes at national scale

**Blockers**:

- Insufficient enforcement resources at LPA level for 30-year monitoring
- Metric calculations that allow "trading down" from high-quality to low-quality habitats
- Lack of transparency in credit transactions and outcomes
- Regulatory capture by development industry interests in platform design

**Related Stakeholders**: Natural England (scientific authority), LPAs (enforcement), DEFRA Policy (regulatory framework), NBN (monitoring data)

## Driver-to-Goal Mapping

### Goal G-1: Functioning Credit Marketplace by Small Sites Mandate

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-1, SD-4, SD-5

**Goal Owner**: SRO, BNG Programme

**Goal Statement**: Deliver a fully operational biodiversity credit marketplace with real-time credit search, purchase, and registration capabilities at least 3 months before the small sites BNG mandate comes into force.

**Why This Matters**: Without a functioning marketplace, small site developers (the majority of planning applications) will have no viable route to BNG compliance, creating a national bottleneck in planning decisions that would be politically catastrophic.

**Success Metrics**:

- **Primary Metric**: Platform live and processing credit transactions before small sites mandate
- **Secondary Metrics**:
  - Average credit purchase-to-confirmation time < 5 working days
  - Credit registry uptime > 99.9% during planning authority business hours
  - Developer satisfaction score > 7/10 in post-transaction survey

**Baseline**: No digital marketplace exists; current BNG compliance is manual and ad hoc

**Target**: Fully digital credit marketplace handling 100% of statutory credit transactions

**Measurement Method**: Platform analytics, transaction logs, user satisfaction surveys

**Dependencies**:

- Biodiversity Metric 4.0 calculation engine validated by Natural England
- Credit validation workflow agreed with Natural England
- LPA integration pathway defined

**Risks to Achievement**:

- Metric calculation complexity delays development
- Insufficient habitat bank supply at platform launch

### Goal G-2: Transparent and Auditable Metric Calculations

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-2, SD-6

**Goal Owner**: Natural England Chief Scientist

**Goal Statement**: Ensure 100% of Biodiversity Metric 4.0 calculations on the platform are fully transparent, with step-by-step audit trails that enable independent verification by qualified ecologists within 30 minutes.

**Why This Matters**: Scientific credibility of the entire BNG policy depends on the metric implementation being demonstrably accurate and transparent. A "black box" calculation would undermine trust across all stakeholder groups.

**Success Metrics**:

- **Primary Metric**: 100% of metric calculations include full audit trail
- **Secondary Metrics**:
  - Independent ecological audit finds < 0.1% calculation error rate
  - Ecologist verification time < 30 minutes per assessment
  - Zero instances of undetected metric manipulation in first 12 months

**Baseline**: Manual spreadsheet-based Metric 4.0 calculations with no audit trail

**Target**: Fully automated, auditable calculations with version-controlled parameters

**Measurement Method**: Independent ecological audit (quarterly), error rate monitoring, user research with ecologists

### Goal G-4: Credible 30-Year Habitat Management Accountability

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-5, SD-6

**Goal Owner**: DEFRA Policy Lead

**Goal Statement**: Establish a digital mechanism for tracking and enforcing 30-year habitat management obligations, with automated monitoring triggers and financial security requirements, within 12 months of platform launch.

**Why This Matters**: The 30-year management obligation is what distinguishes BNG from previous environmental mitigation approaches. Without credible long-term accountability, the policy will fail to deliver lasting biodiversity gains and conservation organisations will withdraw support.

**Success Metrics**:

- **Primary Metric**: 100% of BNG obligations registered with 30-year management plans and financial security
- **Secondary Metrics**:
  - Automated monitoring reminders issued at prescribed intervals
  - Financial security (bonds/insurance) in place for all registered obligations
  - Annual compliance reporting rate > 95%

**Baseline**: No systematic tracking of habitat management obligations

**Target**: Complete digital lifecycle management from obligation creation to 30-year discharge

**Measurement Method**: Registry data, compliance reporting rates, enforcement action records

### Conflict Analysis

**Competing Drivers**:

- **Conflict 1**: Housing developers (SD-4) want speed and simplicity in BNG compliance, but Natural England (SD-2) and conservation charities (SD-6) want rigorous ecological assessment that takes time.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Tiered assessment — automated fast-track for standard habitat types with manual review only for complex/sensitive sites. This satisfies speed requirements for 80% of applications while preserving rigour where it matters most.

- **Conflict 2**: Habitat banks (SD-5) want market flexibility and pricing transparency, but DEFRA Policy wants to use statutory credit pricing as a policy lever to incentivise on-site delivery, which suppresses private market prices.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Clear segmentation between statutory credits (DEFRA, deliberately high-priced) and private market credits (habitat banks, competitively priced) with transparent information about both options. Platform provides market data without price-fixing.

- **Conflict 3**: LPAs (SD-3) want zero additional cost, but robust BNG enforcement requires investment in monitoring and compliance capacity over 30 years.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Phased approach — platform automates initial compliance checks (low cost to LPAs), with DEFRA funding for initial years of 30-year monitoring capacity. Long-term sustainable funding model to be developed for OBC.

**Synergies**:

- **Synergy 1**: Secretary of State's desire for environmental leadership (SD-1) and conservation charities' demand for genuine outcomes (SD-6) are mutually reinforcing — strong platform credibility supports both political and environmental objectives.
- **Synergy 2**: Developer desire for predictability (SD-4) and LPA desire for efficiency (SD-3) align — a streamlined digital process benefits both parties.

**Primary Message**: The BNG Platform provides a clear, predictable pathway to BNG compliance that integrates with your existing planning submission workflow and provides real-time credit availability.

**Key Talking Points**:
