## Document Purpose

This SOBC presents the case for a data-driven spatial planning and development control system, following the HM Treasury Green Book five-case model.

---

## Executive Summary

**Purpose**: Modernise the English planning system through data-driven tools that automate constraint checking, integrate heritage data, provide NPPF policy analysis, and publish structured planning data — enabling planning officers to process applications faster with better evidence.

**Problem Statement**: Local planning authorities process 450,000+ applications annually with 25% officer vacancy rates, 40% staff reduction since 2010, and largely manual processes for checking 15-20 constraint sources per application. Heritage constraint oversights cause an estimated GBP 2 billion in heritage crime damage annually. Only 14 LPAs publish structured planning data despite the Levelling Up and Regeneration Act 2023 data duties.

**Proposed Solution**: A planning analytics platform providing automated constraint checking (heritage, flood, ecology, highways), NPPF policy relevance analysis, statutory consultee workflow management, and structured data publication in DLUHC data standard format.

**Strategic Fit**: Supports housing delivery targets (300,000 homes/year), NPPF implementation, Levelling Up and Regeneration Act 2023 planning data duties, and historic environment protection.

**Investment Required**: GBP 12M over 3 years

- Capital: GBP 8M
- Operational (3 years): GBP 4M

**Expected Benefits**: GBP 38M over 5 years

- Planning officer time savings: GBP 22M (2-3 hours saved per application across 450K applications)
- Reduced heritage crime through better constraint identification: GBP 10M
- Structured data enabling policy monitoring and transparency: GBP 6M

**Return on Investment**:

- NPV: GBP 19.8M (discounted at 3.5%)
- Payback Period: 18 months
- ROI: 217%

**Recommended Option**: Option 2: Integrated Planning Analytics Platform

**Key Risks**:

1. LPA adoption (mitigation: pathfinder programme, integration with existing back-office systems)
2. Heritage data quality insufficient for automated checking (mitigation: Project 004 NHLE digitisation)
3. Resistance from planning profession (mitigation: positioned as decision-support, not decision-making)

**Go/No-Go Recommendation**: **PROCEED**

# PART A: STRATEGIC CASE

## A1. Strategic Context

### A1.1 Problem Statement

**Current Situation**: The English planning system is under severe strain. Planning officer numbers have declined 40% since 2010, with 25% of posts currently vacant. Officers manually check 15-20 constraint sources per application, taking 2-3 hours each time. Heritage constraint oversights occur in an estimated 3-5% of cases. Only 14 of 330+ LPAs publish structured planning data.

**Consequences of Inaction**:
- Housing delivery continues to fall short of 300,000/year target
- Heritage damage from planning oversights continues at GBP 2B/year
- Levelling Up and Regeneration Act planning data duties unmet
- Planning officer burnout and attrition accelerates

### A1.2 Strategic Drivers

| Driver ID | Stakeholder | Driver | Strategic Imperative |
|-----------|-------------|--------|---------------------|
| SD-1 | Minister | Accelerate housing delivery | 300,000 homes/year target |
| SD-2 | LPAs | Do more with less | 25% vacancy rate |
| SD-3 | Historic England | Heritage protection through data | GBP 2B heritage crime |
| SD-4 | Community groups | Meaningful participation | Democratic accountability |

### A1.3 Scope

**In Scope**: Constraint checking, heritage integration, NPPF analysis, consultee workflow, data publication, community consultation portal.
**Out of Scope**: Automated planning decisions, building control, enforcement case management, planning appeals.
