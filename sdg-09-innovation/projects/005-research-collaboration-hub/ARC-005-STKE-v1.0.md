## Executive Summary

### Purpose

This document identifies key stakeholders for the Research Collaboration Hub — a platform connecting UK research institutions with industry partners to accelerate knowledge transfer, commercialisation, and collaborative R&D.

### Key Findings

The Research Collaboration Hub addresses a persistent gap in the UK innovation ecosystem: while UK universities produce world-leading research, translation into commercial products and economic impact lags behind competitor nations. The strongest alignment exists between DSIT, UKRI, and Innovate UK around the policy goal of improving knowledge exchange. The most significant tension is between academic culture (which values open publication) and industrial partners (who want exclusive IP access), with universities caught between maximising REF impact scores and generating commercial revenue from knowledge transfer.

### Critical Success Factors

- Build a critical mass of research profiles and industry opportunities — the platform only works if both sides actively participate
- Integrate with existing systems (ORCID, Gateway to Research, institutional CRIS systems) rather than creating yet another profile for researchers to maintain
- Deliver genuine matchmaking value through research expertise discovery — not just a directory, but intelligent matching of industry needs to academic capabilities
- Navigate IP and confidentiality sensibly — enable initial discovery openly while protecting pre-commercial details
- Demonstrate measurable improvement in knowledge exchange metrics (KE Concordat, HEBCIS returns)

### Stakeholder Alignment Score

**Overall Alignment**: MEDIUM

Strong policy alignment on improving knowledge exchange. Practical tensions between academic open science values and industrial IP protection needs, between institutional autonomy and national platform standardisation, and between DSIT's desire for comprehensive metrics and researchers' resistance to more administrative reporting.

## Stakeholder Drivers Analysis

### SD-1: DSIT — Accelerate Research-to-Market Translation

**Stakeholder**: DSIT Director of Research and Innovation

**Driver Category**: STRATEGIC / POLITICAL

**Driver Statement**: Increase the rate at which UK university research translates into commercial products, services, and economic impact, addressing the persistent "valley of death" between discovery and commercialisation that sees the UK ranked highly for research quality but lower for innovation output.

**Context & Background**:
The UK ranks 4th globally for research quality (Nature Index) but 10th for innovation output (Global Innovation Index). DSIT's R&D framework targets increasing business R&D investment to 2.4% of GDP. Knowledge exchange between universities and industry is a key mechanism, but current processes are fragmented, reliant on personal networks, and inaccessible to SMEs who lack established university relationships.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Knowledge Exchange Framework (KEF) creating institutional incentives
- Innovate UK programmes funding collaborative R&D
- Growing university appetite for industrial engagement (REF impact agenda)

**Blockers**:

- Academic culture prioritises publication over commercialisation
- Industry (especially SMEs) finds universities difficult to engage
- IP negotiation is slow and complex, deterring smaller companies

**Related Stakeholders**: UKRI, Innovate UK, Universities, Industry

---

### SD-2: Researchers — Find Industry Partners Without the Bureaucracy

**Stakeholder**: Academic researchers

**Driver Category**: PERSONAL / OPERATIONAL

**Driver Statement**: Connect with relevant industry partners for collaborative research, consultancy, and knowledge exchange without navigating institutional bureaucracy, with clear support for IP negotiation and contract management.

**Context & Background**:
Researchers who want to work with industry face a complex process: identifying potential partners (often reliant on personal networks or conference contacts), navigating institutional knowledge exchange offices, negotiating IP terms, and managing contracts. Early-career researchers and those at less research-intensive universities are particularly disadvantaged as they lack established industry networks.

**Driver Intensity**: MEDIUM

**Enablers**:

- Researcher profiles already exist in ORCID and institutional CRIS systems — avoid duplicate entry
- Successful KE examples provide models and templates
- REF impact case studies incentivise engagement

**Blockers**:

- Time pressure — researchers already overloaded with teaching, research, and administration
- Concern about IP — who owns jointly created IP?
- Academic promotion criteria still primarily value publications over industry engagement

**Related Stakeholders**: Universities, Knowledge Transfer Offices, Industry

---

### SD-3: Industry (SMEs) — Access University Expertise Without the Complexity

**Stakeholder**: Small and Medium Enterprises

**Driver Category**: COMMERCIAL / OPERATIONAL

**Driver Statement**: Easily find and engage relevant academic expertise for R&D challenges, product development, and innovation without the complexity, cost, and time required by current university engagement processes, which are designed for large companies with dedicated R&D departments.

**Context & Background**:
SMEs represent 99.9% of UK businesses and are key drivers of innovation. However, current university-industry engagement models are optimised for large corporates who have R&D teams, legal departments, and existing university relationships. SMEs report that finding the right researcher, understanding what a university can offer, and navigating the engagement process takes months — by which time the market opportunity has often passed.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Digital platform can make university expertise searchable and discoverable
- Innovate UK voucher schemes can fund initial engagements
- Catapult Centres provide intermediary support

**Blockers**:

- SMEs may not articulate R&D needs in academic language
- University engagement processes assume large-company timescales and budgets
- IP frameworks designed for large contracts, not small engagements

**Related Stakeholders**: FSB, KTN, Catapult Centres, Innovate UK

---

### SD-4: Research England — Improve Knowledge Exchange Metrics

**Stakeholder**: Research England

**Driver Category**: STRATEGIC / COMPLIANCE

**Driver Statement**: Improve measurable knowledge exchange performance across the UK higher education sector as tracked by the Knowledge Exchange Framework (KEF) and HEBCIS returns, demonstrating that public investment in universities generates economic and social impact.

**Context & Background**:
Research England administers the Knowledge Exchange Concordat and KEF, which assess institutional KE performance. Current data collection relies on HEBCIS returns — an annual, retrospective survey. A platform that facilitates and tracks KE activity would provide richer, more timely data on the health of university-industry collaboration nationally.

**Driver Intensity**: MEDIUM

**Enablers**:

- KEF creates institutional incentive for better KE performance
- HEBCIS data infrastructure provides baseline metrics
- KE Concordat sets expectations for institutional KE strategy

**Blockers**:

- Universities resistant to additional reporting requirements
- Attribution of KE outcomes to specific platform interactions is methodologically difficult
- Lag between collaboration initiation and measurable impact (often 3-5 years)

**Related Stakeholders**: Universities, UKRI, HESA/Jisc

### Conflict Analysis

**Competing Drivers**:

- **Conflict 1**: Researchers (SD-2) value open science and publication, but industry partners (SD-3) want exclusive access to pre-commercial research insights. Tension over what information is public vs gated.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Two-tier profile model — public profile shows published research expertise and interests; gated section (accessible after NDA/engagement agreement) shows current projects, available IP, and pre-publication work.

- **Conflict 2**: Universities want to maximise commercial revenue from IP, but SMEs (SD-3) cannot afford complex IP negotiations and high licence fees.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Standardised model agreements for common engagement types (consultancy, sponsored research, licence) with pre-agreed terms for SME engagements. Innovate UK co-funding to subsidise SME access.

## Risk Register (Stakeholder-Related)

### Risk R-1: Low Researcher Adoption

**Related Stakeholders**: Researchers, Universities

**Risk Description**: Researchers do not create or maintain profiles, viewing the platform as yet another administrative burden, resulting in an incomplete directory that fails to attract industry users.

**Probability**: HIGH | **Impact**: HIGH

**Mitigation Strategy**: Integrate with ORCID and CRIS systems to auto-populate profiles; demonstrate career benefit (REF impact, industry funding); minimal manual effort required; institutional KE office champions

---

### Risk R-2: Platform Becomes a Directory, Not a Matchmaker

**Related Stakeholders**: DSIT, Industry partners

**Risk Description**: The platform provides a searchable directory but fails to deliver intelligent matchmaking, becoming indistinguishable from existing resources (Gateway to Research, university websites) and failing to generate new collaborations.

**Probability**: MEDIUM | **Impact**: HIGH

**Mitigation Strategy**: Invest in matchmaking algorithms and curation; employ human brokers (via KTN partnership) alongside algorithmic matching; measure successful matches, not just profile views
