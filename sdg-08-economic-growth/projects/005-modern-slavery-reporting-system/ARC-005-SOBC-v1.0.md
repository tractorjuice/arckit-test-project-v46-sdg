## Document Purpose

This SOBC assesses the case for investing in a Modern Slavery Transparency Compliance platform to replace the current GOV.UK Modern Slavery Statement Registry with a structured data system supporting compliance monitoring, supply chain risk analysis, and law enforcement intelligence sharing.

---

## Executive Summary

**Purpose**: Replace the current PDF-based Modern Slavery Statement Registry with an intelligent platform that enables structured data submission, automated compliance monitoring, cross-referenced supply chain risk analysis, and secure data sharing with law enforcement.

**Problem Statement**: The Modern Slavery Act 2015 (section 54) requires approximately 17,000 organisations with GBP 36M+ turnover to publish annual transparency statements. The current registry is a PDF repository — it cannot analyse, compare, or systematically review statements. The Independent Anti-Slavery Commissioner has called it a "tick-box exercise." Estimated compliance rate is only 60-70%, with no automated mechanism to identify or escalate non-compliance. Law enforcement has no structured way to use statement data for intelligence.

**Proposed Solution**: A dual-classification platform with: (1) a public OFFICIAL compliance system for structured statement submission, compliance monitoring, and transparency dashboard; and (2) a restricted OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE intelligence platform for NCA and law enforcement data sharing and supply chain risk analysis.

**Strategic Fit**: Directly enforces the Modern Slavery Act 2015. Supports the UK Government's Modern Slavery Strategy. Aligns with the Independent Anti-Slavery Commissioner's recommendations. Contributes to SDG 8 (decent work) and SDG 16 (peace, justice, and strong institutions).

**Investment Required**: GBP 10M over 3 years

- Capital: GBP 7M
- Operational (3 years): GBP 3M

**Expected Benefits**: GBP 30M over 5 years

- Compliance improvement (deterrence value and enforcement efficiency): GBP 15M
- Law enforcement efficiency (targeted investigations): GBP 8M
- Business compliance cost reduction (structured submission simpler than PDF): GBP 5M
- Reputational value (UK leadership in anti-slavery): GBP 2M (estimated international influence value)

**Return on Investment**:

- NPV: GBP 14M (discounted at 3.5%)
- Payback Period: 28 months
- ROI: 200%

**Recommended Option**: Option 2: Structured Compliance Platform with Law Enforcement Gateway

**Key Risks**:

1. Business resistance to structured data submission (perceived as increased burden)
2. Dual-classification architecture complexity (OFFICIAL and OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE)
3. Risk scoring methodology challenged as unfair or discriminatory by industry

**Go/No-Go Recommendation**: **PROCEED**

**Rationale**: The current system is not fit for purpose — it fails both transparency (cannot analyse statements) and enforcement (cannot identify non-compliance). The Modern Slavery Act created a reporting obligation without the tools to make reporting meaningful. This investment corrects that gap. The IASC, NCA, and NGO community have all called for this capability. The dual-classification architecture addresses the legitimate need for both public transparency and law enforcement confidentiality.

# PART A: STRATEGIC CASE

## A1. Strategic Context

### A1.1 Problem Statement

**Current Situation**:
The GOV.UK Modern Slavery Statement Registry is essentially a file store. Organisations upload PDF statements of varying quality — from detailed, multi-page documents with supply chain maps to single-page statements that merely assert compliance without evidence. The registry cannot:
- Determine how many in-scope organisations have not submitted (no cross-reference with Companies House)
- Compare statements across years or sectors
- Identify supply chain risk patterns
- Share structured data with law enforcement
- Assess statement quality systematically

**Consequences of Inaction**:

- Modern slavery continues undetected in UK supply chains — estimated 100,000 victims in the UK
- Non-compliant organisations face no effective consequence — the Act has no penalties for non-submission
- The IASC's Annual Report continues to criticise government for inadequate registry
- NCA lacks structured supply chain intelligence for investigations
- UK credibility as leader in anti-slavery policy undermined
- Civil society cannot hold businesses accountable without comparable data

|-----------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| SD-1 | IASC | STRATEGIC | Enable analytical oversight of modern slavery compliance |

**Strategic Alignment**:

- **Modern Slavery Act 2015**: Section 54 transparency obligation — this platform enforces it
- **UK Government Modern Slavery Strategy**: "Use technology and data to identify and disrupt modern slavery"
- **National Action Plan on Business and Human Rights**: Strengthen business accountability mechanisms
