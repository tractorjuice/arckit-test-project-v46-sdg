## Executive Summary

### Purpose

This document identifies key stakeholders for the Universal Credit Modernisation programme, their underlying drivers (motivations, concerns, pressures), how these drivers manifest into goals, and the measurable outcomes that will satisfy those goals. This analysis ensures stakeholder alignment and provides traceability from individual concerns to programme success metrics.

### Key Findings

The UC Modernisation programme faces a classic tension between Ministerial urgency for visible citizen impact and the operational reality of modernising a system that processes over £36 billion annually for approximately 6 million households. The strongest alignment exists around improving claimant experience and reducing processing times — these goals satisfy political, operational, and citizen drivers simultaneously. The most significant conflict is between Treasury's cost-containment pressure and the scale of investment required for genuine modernisation (not just incremental patching).

### Critical Success Factors

- Maintain uninterrupted benefit payments throughout the modernisation — any disruption to payments is a programme-ending event
- Demonstrate measurable improvement to claimant experience within 12 months to maintain political sponsorship
- Achieve GDS service assessment pass at Beta to maintain CDDO confidence and unlock continued funding
- Deliver interoperability with HMRC Real Time Information and local authority systems to enable accurate, timely assessments
- Build internal DWP digital capability to reduce reliance on external suppliers for ongoing operation

### Stakeholder Alignment Score

**Overall Alignment**: MEDIUM

Strong alignment on the need for modernisation and improved claimant experience, but significant tensions between speed of delivery (political pressure), scale of investment (Treasury), operational risk appetite (UC Operations), and architectural ambition (DWP Digital). The devolved administrations add complexity through divergent policy requirements (e.g., Scottish choices on UC).

## Stakeholder Drivers Analysis

### SD-1: Secretary of State — Demonstrable Improvement to Citizen Experience

**Stakeholder**: Secretary of State for Work and Pensions

**Driver Category**: POLITICAL / STRATEGIC

**Driver Statement**: Deliver visible, measurable improvements to the Universal Credit claimant experience that demonstrate the government's commitment to supporting vulnerable people, enabling positive responses to parliamentary questions and media scrutiny.

**Context & Background**:
Universal Credit has been subject to sustained parliamentary and media criticism since its inception — delays, five-week wait, digital exclusion, and complexity. The Secretary of State needs demonstrable progress to maintain political support for the programme and respond to Select Committee inquiries. The SDG 1 alignment creates additional international scrutiny via UN reporting.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Rapid, visible improvements to claimant-facing journeys (quick wins within 6 months)
- Real-time dashboard showing claimant satisfaction and processing times
- Positive case studies and user testimonials for Ministerial use

**Blockers**:

- Slow delivery due to legacy system complexity
- Negative media coverage during transition period
- Operational incidents that undermine confidence in the modernisation

**Related Stakeholders**: Permanent Secretary (accountability), NAO (value for money), Citizens Advice (advocacy), UC Claimants (beneficiaries)

---

### SD-2: DWP Permanent Secretary — Governance, Value for Money, and Accountability

**Stakeholder**: DWP Permanent Secretary (Accounting Officer)

**Driver Category**: RISK / COMPLIANCE

**Driver Statement**: Ensure the UC Modernisation programme demonstrates proper governance, value for money, and accounting officer accountability — avoiding NAO criticism, Public Accounts Committee scrutiny, and ministerial direction requests.

**Context & Background**:
As Accounting Officer, the Permanent Secretary is personally accountable to Parliament for DWP spend. Previous large government IT programmes (Universal Credit original delivery, NHS NPfIT) have attracted severe NAO criticism. The Permanent Secretary needs confidence that the programme has robust governance, realistic plans, and clear value for money evidence. A ministerial direction (where the Minister overrides the Accounting Officer's advice) would be a significant reputational and governance event.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Strong programme governance with clear decision rights and escalation paths
- Robust business case with quantified benefits and credible cost estimates
- Independent assurance reviews (IPA gateway reviews) with green/amber ratings
- Clear risk register with named owners and active mitigation

**Blockers**:

- Unrealistic timelines driven by political pressure
- Insufficient contingency budget for a programme of this scale
- Lack of internal digital capability requiring heavy reliance on external suppliers

**Related Stakeholders**: HM Treasury (funding), NAO (audit), CDDO (assurance), SRO (delivery)

---

### SD-3: UC Operations Director — Continuity of Payments and Staff Capability

**Stakeholder**: UC Operations Director

**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL / RISK

**Driver Statement**: Maintain uninterrupted benefit payments to 6 million households during modernisation while ensuring frontline staff (Work Coaches, Service Centre agents) can operate effectively on the new platform without extended productivity dips.

**Context & Background**:
UC Operations processes approximately 1.2 million changes of circumstances per month, over 300,000 new claims per quarter, and disburses over £3 billion monthly. Any disruption to payments would cause immediate hardship to vulnerable families and generate national media coverage. The 2020 COVID surge (UC claims increased 7x in weeks) demonstrated that the current system struggles under extreme load. Staff in Jobcentres and Service Centres need stable, performant tools — every minute of system downtime is a claimant who cannot be served.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Phased migration with parallel running and rollback capability
- Comprehensive staff training programme with protected learning time
- Realistic cutover plans with go/no-go gates and rehearsals
- Performance and availability equal to or better than current system from day one

**Blockers**:

- Big-bang migration approach with no rollback
- Insufficient training budget or time for 25,000+ operational staff
- Performance regression during transition
- Requirement to maintain two systems during extended parallel running (double operational cost)

**Related Stakeholders**: Work Coaches (users), Contact Centre Management (telephony), Secretary of State (reputational risk), Claimants (direct impact)

---

### SD-4: DWP CDIO — Modern Architecture and Digital Talent

**Stakeholder**: DWP Chief Digital Information Officer

**Driver Category**: STRATEGIC

**Driver Statement**: Deliver a modern, scalable digital architecture that positions DWP as an attractive employer for digital talent, reduces technical debt, and provides a platform for future policy delivery at pace.

**Context & Background**:
DWP Digital is one of the largest government digital functions (over 5,000 staff), but competes with private sector for talent. The current UC platform, while functional, has accumulated significant technical debt. The CDIO needs the modernisation to demonstrate architectural excellence — attracting and retaining skilled engineers, designers, and product people. A modern platform also enables faster implementation of policy changes (currently taking months; target is weeks).

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Architectural freedom to make genuine technology choices (not constrained to incumbent suppliers)
- Investment in internal capability (hiring, training, DDaT career paths)
- Cloud-native architecture enabling modern development practices
- Open source contribution and community engagement

**Blockers**:

- Procurement constraints that favour incumbent suppliers
- Risk-averse governance that prevents adoption of modern patterns
- Insufficient investment in capability building (training, conferences, communities of practice)
- Short-term political pressure overriding long-term architectural quality

**Related Stakeholders**: DDaT Profession Lead (capability), Product Manager (feature velocity), CDDO (standards compliance)

### SD-5: HM Treasury — Cost Control and Value for Money

**Stakeholder**: HM Treasury Spending Team (DWP portfolio)

**Driver Category**: FINANCIAL

**Driver Statement**: Ensure the UC Modernisation programme delivers measurable savings or efficiency improvements that justify the investment, with costs contained within approved spending review allocations and no unplanned calls on the reserve.

**Context & Background**:
Treasury approved the UC Modernisation business case within the Spending Review settlement. They expect rigorous cost control, evidence-based benefits realisation, and no scope creep. Previous large government IT programmes have overrun significantly (UC original programme was several billion over initial estimates). Treasury will scrutinise quarterly spend reports and benefits realisation tracking.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Clear benefits realisation plan with baselined metrics and quarterly reporting
- Strong cost control with earned value management
- Phased delivery enabling incremental benefits capture
- Competitive procurement driving supplier value

**Blockers**:

- Scope creep driven by policy changes mid-programme
- Overreliance on expensive contractor and supplier resource
- Benefits that are qualitative (better claimant experience) rather than quantifiable (FTE savings)
- Inflation and market rate increases for digital skills

**Related Stakeholders**: DWP Finance Director (budget management), NAO (value for money audit), Permanent Secretary (accounting officer)

### SD-6: UC Claimants — Simple, Fast, Dignified Access to Entitlements

**Stakeholder**: Universal Credit Claimants (approximately 6 million households)

**Driver Category**: CUSTOMER / USER

**Driver Statement**: Access Universal Credit entitlements through a simple, fast, dignified process that works on any device, minimises the information burden on claimants, provides clear status updates, and pays the correct amount on time.

**Context & Background**:
UC claimants include single parents, people with disabilities, carers, people who have recently lost employment, and working families on low income. Many face digital exclusion (limited internet access, low digital literacy, no access to devices), have accessibility needs, or are in crisis situations (domestic abuse, homelessness, bereavement). The current UC journal and online claim process, while functional, has been criticised for complexity, unclear status messaging, and the five-week wait for first payment.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Plain language, accessible design following GOV.UK patterns
- Assisted digital pathways (telephone, face-to-face) for those who cannot self-serve
- Pre-population of data from existing government sources (reducing re-keying burden on claimants)
- Real-time claim status updates and clear payment schedules
- Mobile-first responsive design (most claimants access via smartphone)

**Blockers**:

- Complex eligibility rules that resist simplification
- Five-week wait policy (architecture cannot solve a policy issue, but can mitigate with advances)
- Data quality issues in feeds from HMRC and local authorities causing incorrect assessments
- Digital exclusion barriers (connectivity, devices, digital skills)

**Related Stakeholders**: Citizens Advice (advocacy), Work Coaches (assisted digital), Local Authorities (housing costs), Secretary of State (political accountability)

---

### SD-7: HMRC — Reliable, Accurate Data Exchange

**Stakeholder**: HMRC (Her Majesty's Revenue and Customs)

**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL / COMPLIANCE

**Driver Statement**: Maintain reliable, high-volume data exchange (Real Time Information earnings data) with UC systems, with clear interface specifications, agreed SLAs, and minimal impact on HMRC systems during DWP's modernisation.

**Context & Background**:
HMRC provides Real Time Information (RTI) earnings data to DWP's UC system — this is the primary data feed for calculating UC entitlements for working claimants. The interface processes millions of records monthly. HMRC needs assurance that DWP's modernisation will not disrupt this critical data exchange, will maintain backward compatibility during transition, and will not impose unplanned changes on HMRC systems. HMRC also has its own modernisation priorities and limited capacity for partner integration work.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Clear, versioned interface specifications published well in advance of changes
- Joint integration testing environments with realistic data volumes
- Backward compatibility commitments during transition period
- Dedicated integration governance with named contacts on both sides

**Blockers**:

- Unilateral interface changes without adequate notice
- Testing environments that don't reflect production volumes
- DWP timeline changes that disrupt HMRC's own planning
- Data quality issues blamed on one party without joint resolution process

**Related Stakeholders**: UC Operations (data consumer), DWP CDIO (technical architecture), Claimants (incorrect payments if RTI data is wrong)

### SD-8: ICO — Data Protection Compliance and Transparency

**Stakeholder**: Information Commissioner's Office

**Driver Category**: COMPLIANCE / REGULATORY

**Driver Statement**: Ensure the UC Modernisation programme processes personal data lawfully, fairly, and transparently in compliance with UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, with particular attention to automated decision-making, data minimisation, and the rights of vulnerable data subjects.

**Context & Background**:
UC processes some of the most sensitive personal data in government — income, health conditions, caring responsibilities, housing circumstances, relationship status, and children's details. The ICO has a standing interest in large government programmes that process data about vulnerable people at scale. Automated decision-making in benefits calculation engages UK GDPR Article 22 (rights related to automated decision-making). The ICO expects a thorough DPIA and proactive engagement on data protection by design.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Proactive DPIA engagement before system design is finalised
- Data minimisation demonstrably applied (collect only what is needed)
- Clear privacy notices in plain language at appropriate reading level
- Automated decision-making transparency (explainability of UC calculations)
- Robust data subject rights mechanisms (access, rectification, erasure where applicable)

**Blockers**:

- Scope creep in data collection ("while we're here, let's also collect...")
- Opaque automated decision-making with no human review mechanism
- Cross-departmental data sharing without proper legal basis
- Inadequate security controls leading to a data breach affecting millions

**Related Stakeholders**: DWP SIRO (risk acceptance), DWP Data Protection Officer, Claimants (data subjects), Citizens Advice (advocacy)

### SD-9: CDDO — Standards Compliance and Cross-Government Alignment

**Stakeholder**: Central Digital and Data Office

**Driver Category**: COMPLIANCE / STRATEGIC

**Driver Statement**: Ensure the UC Modernisation programme meets GDS Service Standard, Technology Code of Practice, and cross-government digital standards, and that its architecture enables reuse and interoperability across government.

**Context & Background**:
CDDO provides assurance for major digital programmes through service assessments and spend controls. UC Modernisation is one of the largest digital programmes in government and will be closely scrutinised. CDDO wants to see adherence to the Service Standard (user-centred design, open standards, accessibility), compliance with the Technology Code of Practice, and an architecture that enables data sharing and service integration across departments — supporting the "joined-up government" agenda.

**Driver Intensity**: MEDIUM

**Enablers**:

- Early engagement with GDS assessment team (pre-assessment workshops)
- Architecture aligned to cross-government standards (APIs, data formats, identity)
- User research evidence demonstrating user-centred design
- Open source code publication where possible

**Blockers**:

- Closed, proprietary architecture that prevents cross-government integration
- Failure to conduct adequate user research
- Spend control submissions that lack sufficient technical detail
- Non-compliance with Service Standard at assessment gates

**Related Stakeholders**: GDS Assessment Team (assessment delivery), DWP CDIO (architecture), Service Owner (service design)

### SD-10: Local Authorities — Housing Costs Integration

**Stakeholder**: Local Authorities (approximately 300 councils)

**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL

**Driver Statement**: Maintain efficient, accurate housing cost data exchange with UC systems, enabling timely verification of housing costs and direct payments to landlords where appropriate, without imposing unfunded administrative burden on councils.

**Context & Background**:
Local authorities provide housing costs data to UC and receive direct housing payment instructions. Many councils still rely on batch file exchanges and legacy systems. UC Modernisation could improve this integration — or disrupt it. Councils have limited digital budgets and cannot absorb significant integration rework costs. The housing costs element of UC is one of the most complex and error-prone areas, causing significant claimant distress when housing payments are incorrect.

**Driver Intensity**: MEDIUM

**Enablers**:

- Standard, well-documented APIs replacing batch file exchanges
- Adequate lead time for councils to update their systems (minimum 12 months notice)
- Central funding or support for council-side integration changes
- Joint testing with representative sample of council systems

**Blockers**:

- Unfunded mandates requiring councils to update systems at their own cost
- Short notice of interface changes
- Interface specifications that assume modern council systems (many run legacy)
- No single point of contact for 300+ councils to raise integration issues

**Related Stakeholders**: DLUHC (local government department), UC Operations (housing cost processing), Claimants (payment accuracy), Landlords (direct payments)

---

### SD-11: Devolved Administrations — Policy Divergence Support

**Stakeholder**: Scottish Government, Welsh Government, Northern Ireland Executive

**Driver Category**: COMPLIANCE / POLITICAL

**Driver Statement**: Ensure the modernised UC platform supports devolved policy variations (particularly Scottish choices: split payments, direct housing payments, fortnightly payments, and the Scottish Child Payment interface) without these being treated as second-class requirements.

**Context & Background**:
Scotland has exercised devolved powers to modify UC delivery through "Scottish choices" — including the option for claimants to receive payments fortnightly rather than monthly, and to have housing costs paid directly to landlords by default. The Scottish Child Payment (administered by Social Security Scotland) interfaces with UC data. Welsh Government has signalled interest in similar flexibilities. The modernised platform must support these policy variations as first-class configurable features, not hardcoded exceptions.

**Driver Intensity**: MEDIUM

**Enablers**:

- Policy rules engine that supports regional configuration without code changes
- Early engagement with devolved administrations on requirements
- Joint testing with Social Security Scotland for Scottish Child Payment interface
- Representation on the UC Modernisation Programme Board

**Blockers**:

- Architecture that hardcodes England-only policy assumptions
- Devolved requirements treated as low-priority backlog items
- Insufficient testing of devolved variations
- Political tensions between UK Government and devolved administrations affecting working relationships

**Related Stakeholders**: Secretary of State (UK/devolved relations), UC Operations (operational complexity), Claimants in Scotland/Wales/NI (direct beneficiaries)

---

### SD-12: Citizens Advice — Claimant Advocacy and System Transparency

**Stakeholder**: Citizens Advice (and other welfare rights organisations)

**Driver Category**: CUSTOMER / ADVOCACY

**Driver Statement**: Ensure the modernised UC system is transparent, explainable, and fair — enabling advisers to understand how entitlements are calculated, help claimants navigate the system, and identify systemic issues affecting vulnerable groups.

**Context & Background**:
Citizens Advice is the largest provider of free advice in the UK and handles millions of UC-related enquiries annually. Their advisers need to understand how UC is calculated to help claimants check their entitlement, challenge incorrect decisions, and navigate the system. Citizens Advice also publishes research identifying systemic issues (e.g., the five-week wait impact, digital exclusion). They are a powerful advocate and influencer — their reports receive significant media and parliamentary attention.

**Driver Intensity**: MEDIUM

**Enablers**:

- Clear, explainable calculation breakdowns visible to claimants and advisers
- Published API or data feeds enabling Citizens Advice to track systemic issues
- Regular stakeholder forum with genuine influence on design decisions
- Early sight of changes that will affect claimants

**Blockers**:

- Opaque "black box" calculations that cannot be independently verified
- Changes deployed without notice that disrupt adviser guidance
- Stakeholder engagement that is performative rather than genuinely consultative
- Reduced transparency in the name of security

**Related Stakeholders**: Claimants (beneficiaries), ICO (transparency), Secretary of State (reputational risk from negative CA reports)

## Driver-to-Goal Mapping

### Goal G-1: Reduce Average Claim Processing Time

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-1, SD-3, SD-5, SD-6

**Goal Owner**: UC Service Owner

**Goal Statement**: Reduce average new claim processing time from 23 working days to 10 working days by March 2028, while maintaining assessment accuracy above 95%.

**Why This Matters**: Faster processing directly addresses claimant hardship during the waiting period (SD-6), demonstrates visible improvement for Ministerial messaging (SD-1), reduces operational cost per claim (SD-5), and reduces pressure on Service Centre staff (SD-3).

**Success Metrics**:

- **Primary Metric**: Average new claim processing time (working days)
- **Secondary Metrics**:
  - Assessment accuracy rate (correct payment on first calculation)
  - Claimant contact rate during processing (calls asking "where is my claim?")
  - Advance payment request rate (proxy for financial hardship during wait)

**Baseline**: 23 working days average (current)

**Target**: 10 working days average by March 2028

**Measurement Method**: Automated extraction from case management system, reported weekly to Service Owner dashboard and monthly to Programme Board.

**Dependencies**:

- HMRC RTI data available within 48 hours of employer submission (currently up to 7 days for some employers)
- Local authority housing cost verification response time reduced from 10 days to 3 days
- Staff trained on new assessment workflows before rollout

### Conflict Analysis

**Competing Drivers**:

- **Conflict 1**: Secretary of State (SD-1) wants rapid visible improvements versus UC Operations Director (SD-3) needs careful, low-risk migration
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Phased approach — deliver citizen-facing UX improvements on the existing platform first (quick wins visible within 6 months) while the deeper modernisation proceeds at a safe pace. This gives the Minister early results without rushing the migration. Create a "Quick Wins" workstream running in parallel with the "Platform Modernisation" workstream.

- **Conflict 2**: HM Treasury (SD-5) wants cost containment versus DWP CDIO (SD-4) needs significant investment in modern architecture and capability
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Frame the investment as cost avoidance — quantify the cost of maintaining the legacy platform over 10 years versus the cost of modernisation. Show that policy change velocity (O-3) reduces future implementation costs. Present a credible 5-year total cost of ownership comparison in the business case. Accept that some benefits are qualitative (talent retention) and present them alongside quantified savings.

- **Conflict 3**: CDDO (SD-9) wants open standards and cross-government alignment versus operational pressure to choose fastest path
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Adopt open standards from the start as a non-negotiable architecture principle (aligned with ARC-000-PRIN Principle 4). Demonstrate that open standards do not slow delivery — they prevent future rework. Include CDDO in architecture review from Alpha.

- **Conflict 4**: Devolved Administrations (SD-11) want equal priority for their variations versus programme pressure to simplify scope
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Include devolved administration representatives on the Programme Board. Architect the rules engine for regional configurability from the start (it is far cheaper to design for this than to retrofit). Treat Scottish choices as a "proof of configurability" that validates the architecture.

**Synergies**:

- **Synergy 1**: SD-1 (Ministerial visibility) and SD-6 (claimant experience) and SD-5 (cost reduction) all align on G-1 (faster processing) — faster processing is the single goal that satisfies the most stakeholders simultaneously
- **Synergy 2**: SD-4 (modern architecture) and SD-9 (standards compliance) both drive G-4 (scalable architecture) — CDDO's standards requirements and CDIO's modernisation ambition are naturally aligned
- **Synergy 3**: SD-8 (ICO compliance) and SD-12 (Citizens Advice transparency) both drive G-5 (data protection) — regulatory compliance and citizen advocacy point in the same direction on transparency
- **Synergy 4**: SD-3 (operational continuity) and SD-2 (governance accountability) both drive G-3 (zero disruption) — operations and governance are fully aligned on the paramount importance of payment continuity
