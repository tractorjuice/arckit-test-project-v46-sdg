## Document Purpose

This SOBC presents the case for a cross-government Food Bank Coordination System, following the HM Treasury Green Book Five Case Model. It is informed by ARC-004-STKE-v1.0 (stakeholder analysis) and ARC-004-REQ-v1.0 (requirements).

## Executive Summary

**Purpose**: The UK food bank sector distributed over 3 million emergency food parcels in 2024-25 but operates with no national digital coordination. Referrals are paper-based, surplus food redistribution is under-optimised, and government has no reliable food insecurity data. This business case seeks GBP 28M over 5 years for a coordination platform linking food banks, referral agencies, and surplus food providers.

**Problem Statement**: Over 2,500 food banks and community food organisations across the UK operate independently, using paper referral vouchers and ad hoc surplus food distribution. An estimated 3.6 million tonnes of food are wasted in the UK supply chain annually while food banks frequently run short. No national data exists on food insecurity prevalence, hindering evidence-based policy.

**Proposed Solution**: An opt-in digital platform providing digital referrals, food bank capacity mapping, surplus food matching, and anonymised national reporting. Developed in partnership with Trussell Trust, IFAN, and FareShare with independent governance.

**Strategic Fit**: Supports SDG 1: No Poverty and SDG 2: Zero Hunger. Aligns with DEFRA food waste reduction targets, DWP poverty reduction objectives, and DHSC public health nutrition strategy.

**Investment Required**: GBP 28M over 5 years

- Capital: GBP 18M
- Operational (5 years): GBP 10M

**Expected Benefits**: GBP 82M over 10 years

- Food waste reduction (value of redistributed surplus food): GBP 35M
- Referral efficiency savings (time saved for agencies and food banks): GBP 22M
- Health and social cost avoidance (earlier intervention through wider support referral): GBP 15M
- Government policy improvement (evidence-based interventions enabled by data): GBP 10M

**Return on Investment**:

- NPV: GBP 32M (discounted at 3.5%)
- Payback Period: 44 months
- ROI: 193% over 10 years

**Recommended Option**: Option 2: Opt-In Coordination Platform with Independent Governance

**Key Risks**:

1. Food bank sector non-adoption due to distrust of government — mitigated by independent governance, opt-in model, sector-led design
2. Volunteer digital capability insufficient for platform use — mitigated by extreme simplicity, SMS fallback, offline capability
3. Retailer surplus food data not forthcoming — mitigated by alignment with food waste reporting requirements

**Go/No-Go Recommendation**: **PROCEED**

# PART A: STRATEGIC CASE

## A1. Strategic Context

### A1.1 Problem Statement

**Current Situation**: The UK food bank sector has grown from near-zero in 2010 to over 2,500 food banks and community food organisations distributing millions of food parcels annually. The sector is overwhelmingly volunteer-run and charity-funded. Referrals rely on paper vouchers. Surplus food from retailers reaches food banks through ad hoc donation arrangements and FareShare logistics. No national platform coordinates referrals, stock, or surplus food matching.

**Consequences of Inaction**:

- People in food crisis wait 2-3 days between referral and receiving food (could be same-day with digital referrals)
- Approximately 500,000 tonnes of edible surplus food wasted annually that could reach food banks with better logistics coordination
- Government unable to evidence food insecurity prevalence or target interventions
- Food banks waste volunteer time on manual voucher administration

### A1.2 Strategic Alignment

- **SDG 1: No Poverty / SDG 2: Zero Hunger**: Directly addresses food insecurity as a dimension of poverty
- **DEFRA Resources and Waste Strategy**: Reduce food waste in the supply chain by 50% by 2030
- **DHSC Healthy Start and National Food Strategy**: Improve nutrition access for vulnerable households
- **Architecture Principles (ARC-000-PRIN-v1.0)**: Principles 1 (User-Centred), 8 (Privacy by Design), 4 (Interoperability)

## B2. Options Analysis

### Option 0: Do Nothing

**Costs (5-year)**: GBP 0

**Benefits**: GBP 0

**Recommendation**: **Reject** — Fragmentation worsens, surplus food continues to be wasted, no national data.

---

### Option 1: Grant Funding for Sector-Led Digital Tools

**Description**: Provide grant funding to Trussell Trust and IFAN to develop their own digital referral tools independently.

**Costs (5-year)**: GBP 8M

**Benefits (10-year)**: GBP 25M (modest improvement in two separate networks)

**Pros**: Low central risk, sector-led, respects independence

**Cons**: Two separate systems, no national data, no surplus food integration, independent food banks excluded, no interoperability

**Stakeholder Goals Met**: 25%

**Recommendation**: **Reject** — Perpetuates fragmentation.

---

### Option 2: Opt-In Coordination Platform with Independent Governance (RECOMMENDED)

**Description**: Cross-sector platform with independent governance board (Trussell Trust, IFAN, FareShare, DEFRA observer). Opt-in for food banks. Digital referral, surplus food matching, anonymised reporting. Government funds development but does not operate or control the platform.

**Costs (5-year)**: GBP 28M

**Benefits (10-year)**: GBP 82M

**NPV**: GBP 32M | **Payback**: 44 months | **ROI**: 193%

**Stakeholder Goals Met**: 75%

**Recommendation**: **ACCEPT**

---

### Option 3: Mandatory Government Food Aid Platform

**Description**: Government-operated mandatory food aid system replacing existing food bank referral processes.

**Costs (5-year)**: GBP 55M

**Benefits (10-year)**: GBP 95M

**Pros**: Complete coverage, comprehensive data

**Cons**: Food bank sector would refuse to participate. Politically toxic — government "nationalising food banks" narrative. Contradicts sector independence. Would require legislation. Trussell Trust and IFAN publicly oppose.

**Stakeholder Goals Met**: 15% (sector goals NOT met)

**Recommendation**: **Reject** — Undeliverable. Sector would not participate in a mandatory government-controlled system.

---

## B3. Recommended Option

**Option 2** — the only deliverable option. Sector partnership and independent governance are essential for adoption. NPV GBP 32M with acceptable payback.

# PART E: MANAGEMENT CASE

**Programme Governance**: Independent Programme Board chaired by an independent chair (not government), with Trussell Trust, IFAN, FareShare, DEFRA, DWP, and DHSC representation. Government provides funding and policy liaison but does not control platform operations.

**Key Risks**:

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|------------|--------|------------|
| Sector non-adoption | MEDIUM | CRITICAL | Independent governance, sector co-design, opt-in model, no mandatory data sharing |
| Volunteer digital capability | HIGH | MEDIUM | Extreme simplicity, SMS fallback, offline mode, training programme |
| Retailer surplus data availability | MEDIUM | MEDIUM | Align with DEFRA food waste reporting requirements, retailer CSR incentives |
| Political sensitivity (normalising food banks) | HIGH | HIGH | Clear communications strategy, sector-led messaging, government as enabler not operator |
| Data privacy concerns from service users | HIGH | HIGH | Minimal data collection, anonymisation, consent-based, independent data governance |
