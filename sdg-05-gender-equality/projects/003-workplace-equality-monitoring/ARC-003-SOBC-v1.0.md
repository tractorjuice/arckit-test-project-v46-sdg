## Executive Summary

**Purpose**: Build a platform enabling EHRC to systematically monitor workplace equality compliance, shifting from reactive complaint-handling to proactive, data-driven enforcement and public transparency.

**Problem Statement**: EHRC lacks systematic data infrastructure to identify employers with equality compliance failures. Enforcement targets are identified reactively through media, complaints, and political pressure. No public dashboard enables workers or civil society to scrutinise employer equality records. Public sector PSED compliance is inconsistent with no standardised monitoring.

**Proposed Solution**: A data aggregation and analytics platform with automated compliance risk-scoring (internal), employer self-service reporting, public transparency dashboard, and PSED compliance framework.

**Strategic Fit**: Directly enables EHRC's statutory enforcement mandate under the Equality Act 2010 and supports SDG 5 workplace equality objectives.

**Investment Required**: GBP 5.8M over 3 years

- Capital: GBP 3.5M
- Operational (3 years): GBP 2.3M

**Expected Benefits**: GBP 9.6M over 5 years

- EHRC enforcement efficiency: GBP 2.8M
- Employer reporting burden reduction: GBP 1.9M
- Deterrence effect on equality non-compliance (social value): GBP 3.4M
- PSED compliance improvement (public sector efficiency): GBP 1.5M

**Return on Investment**:

- NPV: GBP 2.4M (discounted at 3.5%)
- Payback Period: 30 months
- ROI: 66%

**Recommended Option**: Option 2: Balanced Monitoring Platform

**Key Risks**:

1. Judicial challenge to risk-scoring algorithm (mitigated by transparency, legal review)
2. Employer resistance to monitoring (mitigated by burden reduction focus)
3. Data quality from heterogeneous sources (mitigated by validation and reconciliation)

**Go/No-Go Recommendation**: **PROCEED**

# PART A: STRATEGIC CASE

## A1. Strategic Context

### A1.1 Problem Statement

**Current Situation**:
EHRC processes approximately 3,000 discrimination inquiries per year but has capacity to conduct only 10-15 formal investigations. Target selection is largely reactive — driven by media coverage, political referrals, and complaint volume rather than systematic data analysis. EHRC cannot currently answer basic questions: "Which 50 employers in the UK have the worst equality compliance record?" or "Which public bodies have not published equality objectives as required by PSED?"

**Consequences of Inaction**:

- Enforcement resources allocated to politically prominent cases rather than greatest equality impact
- No public transparency on employer equality performance beyond gender pay gap
- PSED compliance remains inconsistent across 700+ public bodies with no systematic monitoring
- Employment tribunal discrimination findings not systematically tracked by employer

### A1.2 Strategic Alignment

- **Equality Act 2010**: Enablement of EHRC enforcement powers (Sections 20-24)
- **EHRC Strategic Plan 2022-2025**: "Data-driven enforcement prioritisation"
- **SDG 5**: Workplace gender equality monitoring and accountability
- **ARC-000-PRIN-v1.0**: Principles 10 (Gender-Disaggregated Data), 13 (Intersectionality), 21 (Equality Act Compliance)
