## Document Purpose

This SOBC presents the case for investing in a multi-agency domestic abuse case management system. This is a life-safety programme — the investment case rests on preventing harm and saving lives as much as on financial returns. The business case follows the HM Treasury Green Book five-case model with supplementary social value analysis.

## Executive Summary

**Purpose**: Provide a secure, trauma-informed multi-agency case management platform enabling coordinated domestic abuse risk assessment, safety planning, and information sharing across police, health, social services, and specialist DA services.

**Problem Statement**: The multi-agency response to domestic abuse is fatally undermined by fragmented information systems. Domestic Homicide Reviews repeatedly identify information sharing failures as a contributing factor. MARACs operate on weekly cycles while risk escalates in real-time. Survivors must repeat their traumatic experiences to an average of 5+ agencies.

**Proposed Solution**: A secure multi-agency platform with digital DASH-RIC, real-time information sharing for high-risk cases, survivor consent management, MARAC coordination tools, and aggregate analytics.

**Strategic Fit**: Directly supports the Domestic Abuse Act 2021, the VAWG Strategy 2021, and the UK's SDG 5 commitments on ending violence against women and girls.

**Investment Required**: GBP 14.5M over 3 years

- Capital: GBP 9.2M
- Operational (3 years): GBP 5.3M

**Expected Benefits**: GBP 28.6M over 5 years (including monetised social value)

- Multi-agency efficiency savings: GBP 8.2M
- Reduced repeat victimisation costs: GBP 12.4M (social value)
- Reduced criminal justice costs: GBP 5.8M
- Reduced NHS costs from DA-related presentations: GBP 2.2M

**Return on Investment**:

- NPV: GBP 9.8M (discounted at 3.5%)
- Payback Period: 32 months
- ROI: 97%

**Recommended Option**: Option 2: Phased Multi-Agency Platform

**Key Risks**:

1. Survivor data breach (life-safety risk — mitigated by field-level encryption, specialist security testing)
2. Agency adoption reluctance (mitigated by Ministerial direction, phased rollout)
3. Integration complexity across 43 police forces and 152 local authorities

**Go/No-Go Recommendation**: **PROCEED**

**Rationale**: This is a life-safety programme. Domestic Homicide Reviews repeatedly cite information sharing failures. The cost of inaction is measured in lives lost. The recommended option delivers strong value for money with a positive NPV, and the social value analysis demonstrates substantial wider benefits.

# PART A: STRATEGIC CASE

## A1. Strategic Context

### A1.1 Problem Statement

**Current Situation**:
The multi-agency response to domestic abuse operates across police, health, social services, housing, and specialist DA services with no integrated case management. Each agency maintains separate records. Multi-Agency Risk Assessment Conferences (MARACs) provide a structured sharing mechanism, but they operate on weekly/fortnightly cycles. When a survivor contacts police at 2am on a Saturday, the responding officer has no visibility of the health concerns flagged by an A&E consultant on Thursday, the social services assessment completed on Wednesday, or the safety plan agreed with Women's Aid on Monday.

**Specific Pain Points**:

| Stakeholder | Driver ID | Pain Point | Impact | Intensity |
|-------------|-----------|------------|--------|-----------|
| DA Commissioner | SD-1 | Information sharing failures in DHRs | Lives lost | CRITICAL |
| Police | SD-2 | No multi-agency data at point of response | Risk under-assessed | CRITICAL |
| Survivors | SD-3 | Repeat traumatisation, fear of data exposure | Reluctance to engage, re-victimisation | CRITICAL |
| NHS | SD-4 | Clinicians uncertain about sharing legal basis | Health data not reaching MARAC | HIGH |

**Consequences of Inaction**:

- Continued information sharing failures contributing to domestic homicides (approximately 150 per year in England and Wales)
- Continued re-traumatisation of survivors across multiple agency interactions
- MARAC coordination remaining manual, inefficient, and inconsistent
- Domestic Abuse Commissioner's statutory recommendations unfulfilled

### A1.2 Strategic Alignment

- **Domestic Abuse Act 2021**: Direct enablement of the multi-agency response framework
- **VAWG Strategy 2021**: Technology pillar — improving multi-agency information sharing
- **SDG 5 (Gender Equality)**: Target 5.2 — eliminate all forms of violence against women and girls
- **ARC-000-PRIN-v1.0**: Principles 1 (Trauma-Informed Design), 3 (Survivor Safety), 16 (Multi-Agency Data Sharing)

### A1.5 Why Now?

**Urgency Factors**:

- Domestic Abuse Commissioner's annual report recommends integrated case management within 3 years
- 2025 Domestic Homicide Review thematic analysis identified technology gap as the top systemic failing
- VAWG Strategy committed to technology-enabled multi-agency response by 2027
- COVID-19 legacy: domestic abuse reporting increased 30% — demand on services has not returned to pre-pandemic levels

### Option 3: Full National DA Digital Service

**Description**: Comprehensive national platform including all Option 2 features plus perpetrator tracking integration, family court data sharing, refuge accommodation management, and AI-driven risk prediction.

**Costs** (3-year): GBP 28.5M

**Benefits** (5-year): GBP 35.2M

**Cons**:

# PART E: MANAGEMENT CASE

## E2. Delivery Approach

**Methodology**: Agile with GDS service assessment gates and additional survivor safety gates

**Phases**:

1. **Discovery** (Months 1-4): Survivor research (ethical protocol), multi-agency requirements, legal basis mapping
2. **Alpha** (Months 5-10): Prototype, security architecture, DA Commissioner review, GDS Alpha assessment
3. **Phase 1 Beta** (Months 11-18): MARAC coordination + digital DASH-RIC, pilot with 10 MARAC areas
4. **Phase 2 Beta** (Months 19-26): Real-time sharing + survivor consent, expand to 50 MARAC areas
5. **Phase 3 Beta/Live** (Months 27-36): NHS/LA integration, national rollout to 75%+ MARAC areas
