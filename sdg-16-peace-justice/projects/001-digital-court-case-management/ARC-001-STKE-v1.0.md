## Executive Summary

### Purpose

This document identifies key stakeholders for the Digital Court Case Management system, their underlying drivers (motivations, concerns, pressures), how these drivers manifest into goals, and the measurable outcomes that will satisfy those goals. This analysis ensures stakeholder alignment and provides traceability from individual concerns to programme success metrics.

### Key Findings

The Digital Court Case Management project sits at the intersection of judicial independence and government digital transformation. The strongest alignment exists around reducing the Crown Court backlog (67,000+ cases) and improving case progression — these goals satisfy judicial, ministerial, operational, and citizen drivers simultaneously. The most significant conflict is between the judiciary's requirement for systems that respect judicial independence and discretion, and HMCTS's drive for standardised digital workflows that improve efficiency. Treasury's cost-containment pressure conflicts with the scale of investment required given the GBP 1.3 billion HMCTS Reform Programme context.

### Critical Success Factors

- Maintain uninterrupted court operations during transition — any disruption to hearings is unacceptable
- Achieve judicial buy-in through co-design with judges and magistrates at every stage
- Integrate seamlessly with the Common Platform and existing HMCTS digital services
- Demonstrate measurable reduction in case progression times within 12 months
- Pass GDS service assessment at Beta to maintain CDDO confidence and continued funding

### Stakeholder Alignment Score

**Overall Alignment**: MEDIUM

Strong alignment on the need to reduce the backlog and modernise case management, but significant tensions between judicial independence (judiciary), standardisation and efficiency (HMCTS), cost containment (Treasury), and defence practitioner workflows (Bar Council, Law Society). The Criminal Justice Board adds complexity through cross-agency dependencies (CPS, police, probation, prisons).

## Stakeholder Drivers Analysis

### SD-1: Lord Chancellor — Reducing the Crown Court Backlog and Restoring Public Confidence

**Stakeholder**: Lord Chancellor and Secretary of State for Justice

**Driver Category**: POLITICAL / STRATEGIC

**Driver Statement**: Deliver visible, measurable reduction in the Crown Court case backlog and waiting times, demonstrating the government's commitment to an effective justice system and enabling positive responses to parliamentary questions, media scrutiny, and international reporting on SDG 16.

**Context & Background**:
The Crown Court backlog exceeded 67,000 cases in 2024, with some defendants waiting over two years for trial. This represents a crisis in public confidence in the justice system. The Lord Chancellor faces regular Select Committee questioning on court delays. The HMCTS Reform Programme (GBP 1.3 billion) was expected to deliver efficiency gains that have not yet materialised at the pace required. SDG 16 international reporting creates additional scrutiny on the UK's justice system performance.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Rapid, demonstrable improvements to case progression times (quick wins within 6 months)
- Real-time dashboard showing backlog reduction trajectory and case flow metrics
- Positive case studies showing improved experience for victims, witnesses, and defendants
- Cross-agency cooperation from CPS, police, and HMPPS on case readiness

**Blockers**:

- Slow delivery due to legacy system complexity and Common Platform integration challenges
- Judicial resistance to standardised digital workflows
- CPS case file quality issues that delay hearings regardless of court system improvements
- Budget constraints limiting the number of sitting days available

**Related Stakeholders**: HMCTS CEO (accountability), NAO (value for money), Victims' Commissioner (experience), Lord Chief Justice (judicial cooperation)

---

### SD-2: Lord Chief Justice — Judicial Independence and Effective Case Management

**Stakeholder**: Lord Chief Justice of England and Wales

**Driver Category**: STRATEGIC / CONSTITUTIONAL

**Driver Statement**: Ensure that digital case management systems respect judicial independence and discretion, support effective case management by judges, and do not impose algorithmic or bureaucratic constraints on judicial decision-making.

**Context & Background**:
The judiciary is constitutionally independent from the executive. While HMCTS (an executive agency of MoJ) builds and operates court systems, judges must retain full discretion over case management decisions — listing priorities, adjournment decisions, sentencing, and case directions. The Lord Chief Justice has publicly expressed concern about technology systems that constrain judicial discretion or create a perception that algorithmic tools influence judicial decisions. The Criminal Procedure Rules give judges active case management duties, and the digital system must support rather than constrain these duties.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Co-design approach with judiciary at every stage (not designing for judges, designing with judges)
- Clear separation between workflow support (acceptable) and decision automation (unacceptable)
- Judicial user research with sitting judges across jurisdictions
- Judicial IT Advisory Committee engagement throughout the programme

**Blockers**:

- Perception that the system imposes efficiency-driven constraints on judicial decisions
- Poor usability that slows judicial workflows rather than helping
- Inadequate consultation with the judiciary before deployment
- Systems that generate performance metrics that could be used to assess individual judicial performance

**Related Stakeholders**: Senior Presiding Judge (deployment), Judicial Office (administration), Bar Council (advocacy), Lord Chancellor (constitutional relationship)

---

### SD-3: HMCTS Chief Executive — Operational Efficiency and Reform Delivery

**Stakeholder**: HMCTS Chief Executive (Accounting Officer)

**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL / FINANCIAL

**Driver Statement**: Deliver the case management modernisation within the HMCTS Reform Programme budget and timeline, achieving measurable operational efficiencies that justify continued Treasury investment while maintaining uninterrupted court operations throughout the transition.

**Context & Background**:
HMCTS has invested GBP 1.3 billion in the Reform Programme since 2016. The programme has delivered several digital services (online plea, online civil money claims) but the core case management system remains a patchwork of legacy systems (Libra, XHIBIT, CMS). The HMCTS CEO, as Accounting Officer, is personally accountable to Parliament for the spend. NAO has previously criticised the Reform Programme's pace and benefit realisation. The CEO needs the case management modernisation to demonstrate clear value for money.

**Driver Intensity**: CRITICAL

**Enablers**:

- Phased migration with clear milestones and measurable benefits at each phase
- Strong cost control with earned value management and benefit tracking
- Common Platform as the integration backbone, avoiding duplicate data entry
- Staff training programme with protected learning time and support

**Blockers**:

- Legacy system complexity making migration slower and more expensive than planned
- Judicial requirements adding scope that was not in the original business case
- CPS and police dependencies on Common Platform readiness
- Staff resistance to workflow changes in Service Centres and courts

**Related Stakeholders**: HM Treasury (funding), NAO (audit), CDDO (assurance), Operations Director (delivery)

---

### SD-4: Crown Prosecution Service — Seamless Digital Case File Exchange

**Stakeholder**: Crown Prosecution Service

**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL / INTEGRATION

**Driver Statement**: Achieve seamless, bi-directional digital case file exchange between CPS and HMCTS systems through the Common Platform, eliminating duplicate data entry, reducing case file preparation time, and enabling prosecutors to manage cases efficiently across multiple courts.

**Context & Background**:
CPS prosecutors handle cases across multiple courts simultaneously. The Common Platform was designed to provide a shared digital workspace for prosecutors and court staff, but integration has been challenging. Prosecutors currently maintain information in CPS Case Management System (CMS) and must also interact with HMCTS systems for listing, results, and case progression. Duplicate data entry wastes prosecutor time and introduces inconsistency. Poor data quality in case files is a major contributor to ineffective trial hearings (cracked and ineffective trials).

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Common Platform data standards applied consistently by both HMCTS and CPS systems
- Single data entry point with automated synchronisation
- Prosecutor-friendly interface for managing multi-court caseloads
- Real-time case status visibility across the criminal justice system

**Blockers**:

- Incompatible data models between CPS CMS and HMCTS systems
- Common Platform API stability and performance issues
- Different organisational priorities and release cadences between CPS and HMCTS
- Data quality issues in police case files that propagate through the system

**Related Stakeholders**: HMCTS CDIO (integration), Police (evidence), LAA (defence representation), Lord Chief Justice (case readiness)

---

### SD-5: Bar Council and Law Society — Practitioner Workflow and Professional Access

**Stakeholder**: Bar Council and Law Society (representing barristers and solicitors)

**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL / CUSTOMER

**Driver Statement**: Ensure that the digital case management system provides defence practitioners with efficient access to case materials, court listings, and case progression information, and does not create additional administrative burden on practitioners already under financial pressure from legal aid fee constraints.

**Context & Background**:
Criminal defence practitioners operate under significant financial pressure — legal aid fees have not kept pace with inflation, and many firms have left criminal defence work. Any new digital system that increases administrative burden on practitioners further threatens the viability of criminal defence practice. Practitioners need mobile-friendly access to case materials (often reviewed in court corridors or during travel), clear listing information, and efficient mechanisms for filing documents and communicating with the court.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Mobile-responsive interface that works on practitioners' devices (tablets, phones)
- Single sign-on across HMCTS digital services
- Clear, timely listing notifications with changes highlighted
- Bulk operations for practitioners managing multiple cases
- API access for integration with practice management software

**Blockers**:

- System that requires desktop access only (practitioners are mobile)
- Additional registration or authentication hurdles beyond existing professional accreditation
- Workflow that assumes all users have dedicated administrative support (sole practitioners do not)
- Downtime during court sitting hours

**Related Stakeholders**: LAA (legal aid status), CPS (case disclosure), Lord Chief Justice (practitioner access), Litigants in person (similar access needs without professional tools)

---

### SD-6: Court Users — Timely, Understandable, Accessible Justice

**Stakeholder**: Court users (defendants, witnesses, victims, litigants in person)

**Driver Category**: CUSTOMER / USER

**Driver Statement**: Access court services through a clear, timely, accessible process that keeps them informed of case progress, minimises unnecessary attendance, and provides understandable information about proceedings — regardless of whether they have legal representation.

**Context & Background**:
Court users include defendants (some in custody, some on bail), witnesses (including vulnerable witnesses and victims), family members, and litigants in person in civil and family proceedings. Approximately 40% of civil and family court users are unrepresented. Many court users find the process intimidating, confusing, and opaque. Current systems provide limited visibility into case progress — users often learn about hearing dates through their solicitor or by phoning the court. Victims and witnesses face particular anxiety about attendance requirements and case outcomes.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Clear, plain-language notifications about hearing dates, changes, and outcomes
- Self-service portal for checking case status without phoning the court
- Guided journeys for litigants in person explaining what to expect and what to prepare
- Accessibility features meeting WCAG 2.2 Level AA for all citizen-facing interfaces
- Integration with GOV.UK Notify for multi-channel notifications (email, SMS, letter)

**Blockers**:

- System designed primarily for professional users with legal knowledge assumed
- Digital-only access excluding citizens without internet access or digital skills
- Legal jargon in notifications and interface text
- Inconsistent information between different HMCTS channels (online, phone, in-court)

**Related Stakeholders**: Citizens Advice (support), Victims' Commissioner (experience), Lord Chancellor (public confidence), LAA (representation status)

## Driver-to-Goal Mapping

### Goal G-1: Reduce Crown Court Case Backlog by 20% Within 18 Months

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-1, SD-3, SD-6

**Goal Owner**: HMCTS Chief Executive

**Goal Statement**: Reduce the outstanding Crown Court caseload from 67,000 to below 54,000 within 18 months of full system deployment through improved case progression, automated scheduling optimisation, and reduced cracked and ineffective trials.

**Why This Matters**: Directly addresses the Lord Chancellor's political driver (SD-1), the HMCTS CEO's operational efficiency driver (SD-3), and court users' desire for timely justice (SD-6).

**Success Metrics**:

- **Primary Metric**: Outstanding Crown Court caseload count (monthly)
- **Secondary Metrics**:
  - Average time from charge to trial completion
  - Cracked and ineffective trial rate
  - Case progression compliance rate

**Baseline**: 67,000 outstanding cases (March 2026)
**Target**: Below 54,000 outstanding cases (September 2027)
**Measurement Method**: HMCTS Management Information quarterly statistical bulletin

---

### Goal G-2: Achieve Seamless Common Platform Integration

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-3, SD-4, SD-5

**Goal Owner**: HMCTS Chief Digital and Information Officer

**Goal Statement**: Deliver end-to-end digital case management integrated with the Common Platform, eliminating duplicate data entry for HMCTS staff, CPS prosecutors, and defence practitioners by September 2027.

**Why This Matters**: Addresses CPS integration needs (SD-4), practitioner efficiency (SD-5), and HMCTS operational efficiency (SD-3).

**Success Metrics**:

- **Primary Metric**: Percentage of case data entered once and shared across systems
- **Secondary Metrics**:
  - CPS prosecutor time spent on duplicate data entry (target: zero)
  - Data consistency score between HMCTS and CPS records
  - API uptime for Common Platform integrations

**Baseline**: Approximately 60% of case data requires re-entry across systems
**Target**: 100% single data entry with automated synchronisation
**Measurement Method**: System usage analytics and practitioner survey

---

### Goal G-3: Judicial Satisfaction Score Above 80%

**Derived From Drivers**: SD-2, SD-1

**Goal Owner**: SRO, HMCTS Reform Programme

**Goal Statement**: Achieve a judicial user satisfaction score of 80% or above within 6 months of deployment, measured through structured feedback from judges and magistrates using the system.

**Why This Matters**: Without judicial buy-in (SD-2), the system will fail regardless of its technical quality. Judicial satisfaction also supports the Lord Chancellor's confidence driver (SD-1).

**Success Metrics**:

- **Primary Metric**: Judicial user satisfaction score (quarterly survey)
- **Secondary Metrics**:
  - Judicial adoption rate (percentage using digital vs. paper)
  - Time to complete routine case management tasks (digital vs. baseline)
  - Judicial helpdesk call volume (trending down)

**Baseline**: No current baseline (new system)
**Target**: 80% satisfaction within 6 months, 90% within 12 months
**Measurement Method**: Judicial user survey and system usage analytics

### Conflict Analysis

**Competing Drivers**:

- **Conflict 1**: The Lord Chief Justice (SD-2) wants systems that preserve full judicial discretion over case management, while HMCTS CEO (SD-3) wants standardised workflows that improve efficiency and throughput. Judges may resist automated case progression prompts as inappropriate pressure.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Co-design with judiciary — system provides information and suggestions, never mandates. Automated prompts are advisory only. Judicial discretion is explicitly preserved in all workflow designs. Judicial IT Advisory Committee has sign-off authority on case management workflow design.

- **Conflict 2**: Bar Council/Law Society (SD-5) want minimal additional administrative burden on practitioners, while HMCTS (SD-3) wants comprehensive digital data capture. More data fields improve case management but increase practitioner workload.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Progressive disclosure — minimum mandatory fields with optional enhancement. Auto-population from Common Platform data. API access for practice management software integration to reduce manual entry.

- **Conflict 3**: HM Treasury wants cost containment within approved budgets, while the scale of integration with Common Platform, CPS, police, and HMPPS requires significant investment beyond original estimates.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Phased delivery with incremental benefits realisation at each phase. Clear benefits tracking linked to Treasury spending review evidence. De-scoping of non-essential features to fit budget envelope.

**Synergies**:

- **Synergy 1**: Lord Chancellor's backlog reduction driver (SD-1) aligns perfectly with court users' desire for timely justice (SD-6) — improving case progression satisfies both political and citizen outcomes
- **Synergy 2**: CPS integration needs (SD-4) align with HMCTS efficiency goals (SD-3) — eliminating duplicate data entry benefits both organisations simultaneously
- **Synergy 3**: Practitioner efficiency gains (SD-5) directly support backlog reduction (SD-1) — faster case preparation means more effective hearings

## Communication & Engagement Plan

### Lord Chief Justice and Senior Judiciary

**Primary Message**: This system is designed with judges, for judges — it supports judicial case management duties without constraining judicial discretion.

**Key Talking Points**:

- Co-design approach with judicial user research at every phase
- System provides information and suggestions, never mandates judicial decisions
- No individual judicial performance metrics generated from the system
- Judicial IT Advisory Committee has design approval authority

**Communication Frequency**: Monthly judicial engagement sessions
**Preferred Channel**: In-person sessions at Royal Courts of Justice, written briefings via Judicial Office
**Success Story**: Judge reports that the system helps them identify cases at risk of delay, enabling proactive case management

---

### Legal Practitioners (Bar Council, Law Society)

**Primary Message**: This system reduces your administrative burden and gives you better access to case information — designed around how you actually work.

## Risk Register (Stakeholder-Related)

### Risk R-1: Judicial Rejection of Digital Case Management

**Related Stakeholders**: Lord Chief Justice, Senior Presiding Judge, judiciary
**Risk Description**: Judges reject or minimally adopt the digital system, reverting to paper-based processes and undermining the investment
**Impact on Goals**: G-3 (judicial satisfaction), G-1 (backlog reduction dependent on judicial adoption)
**Probability**: MEDIUM
**Impact**: HIGH
**Mitigation Strategy**: Co-design with judiciary from Discovery, judicial champions programme, phased rollout with feedback loops, maintain paper fallback during transition
**Contingency Plan**: Extended parallel running with paper, targeted one-to-one judicial training

### Risk R-2: Common Platform Integration Delays

**Related Stakeholders**: CPS, HMCTS CDIO, defence practitioners
**Risk Description**: Common Platform APIs are not stable or performant enough for production integration, delaying the elimination of duplicate data entry
**Impact on Goals**: G-2 (integration), G-1 (backlog reduction dependent on efficient data flow)
**Probability**: HIGH
**Impact**: HIGH
**Mitigation Strategy**: Early integration testing, API stability requirements in contract, fallback to manual data transfer if APIs unavailable
**Contingency Plan**: Phased integration starting with read-only data sharing, write operations in later phase

### Risk R-3: Practitioner Disengagement Due to Increased Administrative Burden

**Related Stakeholders**: Bar Council, Law Society, defence practitioners
**Risk Description**: System increases rather than decreases administrative burden on practitioners, leading to professional body complaints and potential refusal to use the system
**Impact on Goals**: G-2 (integration), O-2 (practitioner productivity)
**Probability**: MEDIUM
**Impact**: MEDIUM
**Mitigation Strategy**: Practitioner working group, usability testing with sole practitioners, minimum mandatory fields, API access for practice management integration
**Contingency Plan**: Rapid iteration on practitioner feedback, dedicated practitioner UX sprint
