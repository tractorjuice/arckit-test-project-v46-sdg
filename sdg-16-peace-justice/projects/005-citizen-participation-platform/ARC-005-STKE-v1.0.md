## Executive Summary

### Purpose

This document analyses stakeholders for the Citizen Participation Platform — a digital platform enabling public consultations, petitions, participatory budgeting, and citizen engagement across central and local government, supporting the UK's Open Government Partnership commitments and SDG 16.7 (responsive, inclusive, participatory decision-making).

### Key Findings

The Citizen Participation Platform faces a fundamental challenge: current government consultation processes are perceived as tokenistic — "consult and ignore" — which has eroded public trust in participatory democracy. The strongest alignment exists around the shared recognition that better engagement tools could improve policy quality by incorporating diverse citizen perspectives. The most significant conflict is between government departments wanting to maintain control over consultation design and citizen advocacy groups wanting genuinely deliberative processes that influence outcomes.

### Critical Success Factors

- Demonstrate that citizen input genuinely influences policy outcomes (the "You Said, We Did" principle)
- Achieve participation from demographically diverse communities, not just organised lobby groups
- Reduce the time and cost of running government consultations by 50%
- Integrate with existing GOV.UK consultation publishing workflows
- Comply with the Government Consultation Principles and the Nolan Principles of Public Life

### Stakeholder Alignment Score

**Overall Alignment**: MEDIUM

Strong agreement that current consultation processes are slow, expensive, and fail to reach diverse audiences. Tensions between departments wanting efficient consultation management and citizens wanting meaningful deliberation, between standardised digital tools and bespoke engagement approaches, and between open participation and managing organised lobbying campaigns.

## Stakeholder Drivers Analysis

### SD-1: DLUHC Minister — Revitalising Democratic Engagement

**Stakeholder**: DLUHC Minister

**Driver Category**: POLITICAL / STRATEGIC

**Driver Statement**: Demonstrate that the government is genuinely committed to listening to citizens by delivering a modern participation platform that reaches diverse communities, produces visible evidence of citizen influence on policy, and supports the Levelling Up agenda by giving voice to underrepresented areas and groups.

**Context & Background**:
Public trust in government has declined significantly. The Edelman Trust Barometer 2025 showed UK government trust at 36%. Voter turnout has been falling (67.3% in 2019, 59.9% in 2024). The perception that consultations are tokenistic — pre-determined outcomes with a consultation box-ticking exercise — undermines democratic legitimacy. The Levelling Up agenda explicitly aims to give communities more voice in decisions affecting them. SDG 16.7 requires responsive, inclusive, participatory, and representative decision-making at all levels.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Platform that clearly shows how citizen input influenced outcomes ("You Said, We Did" reports)
- Active outreach to underrepresented communities (digital, face-to-face, community partnerships)
- High-profile consultations demonstrating genuine impact (e.g., planning reform, local service design)
- Positive media coverage of citizen engagement success stories

**Blockers**:

- Departments running consultations with predetermined outcomes (consultation fatigue)
- Platform perceived as digital-only, excluding non-digital communities
- Low participation rates suggesting limited public interest
- Organised lobbying campaigns distorting consultation responses

**Related Stakeholders**: Cabinet Office Consultation Hub (standards), Citizens (participants), Involve (expertise), Local authorities (local engagement)

### SD-2: Cabinet Office Consultation Hub — Standardised, Efficient Consultation Management

**Stakeholder**: Cabinet Office Consultation Hub

**Driver Category**: OPERATIONAL / COMPLIANCE

**Driver Statement**: Standardise consultation processes across government departments, reduce the cost and time of running consultations, ensure compliance with the Government Consultation Principles, and provide consistent data on consultation outcomes.

**Context & Background**:
Government runs approximately 500+ formal consultations per year, plus thousands of informal engagements. Current processes vary widely — some departments use commercial survey tools, others use PDF documents with email responses, and some use bespoke platforms. There is no standard for consultation response analysis, no cross-government reporting on consultation outcomes, and no consistency in how results are published. The Government Consultation Principles require a minimum 12-week consultation period with proper analysis and government response, but compliance is inconsistent.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Single, standardised platform for all government consultations
- Template-based consultation design with built-in compliance checks
- Automated response analysis (including NLP for open-text responses)
- Cross-government reporting on consultation volumes, response rates, and outcomes
- Integration with GOV.UK consultation publishing

**Blockers**:

- Departments preferring their own tools and processes
- Resistance to standardisation from departments with specialised consultation needs
- Low adoption if the platform is less flexible than existing commercial tools
- Template approach perceived as limiting for complex policy consultations

**Related Stakeholders**: CDDO (standards), Departmental policy teams (users), GDS (GOV.UK integration)

### SD-3: Citizens — Meaningful Voice in Government Decisions

**Stakeholder**: Citizens (UK public)

**Driver Category**: CUSTOMER / DEMOCRATIC

**Driver Statement**: Have a meaningful voice in government decisions that affect their lives, through accessible, inclusive participation channels that respect their time, acknowledge their input, and demonstrate how their views influenced outcomes.

**Context & Background**:
Citizens are frustrated by participation processes that appear to have no impact. The 2024 British Social Attitudes survey showed that only 18% of respondents believed government consultations influenced policy. Many citizens — particularly from ethnic minority communities, disabled people, young people, and those in deprived areas — feel excluded from consultation processes. Digital participation tools can reach new audiences but risk excluding those without internet access or digital skills.

**Driver Intensity**: HIGH

**Enablers**:

- Simple, accessible participation interface completable in under 15 minutes
- Clear explanation of what is being decided and how citizen input will be used
- Transparent publication of outcomes showing how responses influenced decisions
- Multi-channel participation (online, SMS, face-to-face, postal for non-digital)
- Language support and Easy Read formats

**Blockers**:

- Complex, jargon-heavy consultation documents
- Digital-only participation excluding non-digital communities
- No feedback on how responses were used (consultation black hole)
- Participation fatigue from too many consultations
- Distrust that input will make any difference

**Related Stakeholders**: Involve (engagement methods), Disability Rights UK (accessibility), Ethnic minority organisations (inclusion), mySociety (civic tech)

### SD-4: Involve and Democratic Society — Genuine Deliberative Democracy

**Stakeholder**: Involve, Democratic Society

**Driver Category**: STRATEGIC / VALUES

**Driver Statement**: Ensure that the platform supports genuine deliberative democracy approaches — not just opinion polling — including citizens' assemblies, participatory budgeting, deliberative surveys, and structured dialogue where citizens engage with evidence and each other before forming views.

**Context & Background**:
Traditional consultations (12-week open comment period) tend to capture organised voices (lobby groups, professional bodies) rather than considered citizen deliberation. Deliberative methods — citizens' assemblies (e.g., Climate Assembly UK), participatory budgeting (e.g., Paris, Lisbon), and structured dialogue — produce more representative and considered outcomes. The UK has experimented with these approaches but has no standard digital infrastructure to support them at scale.

**Driver Intensity**: MEDIUM

**Enablers**:

- Platform supports multiple engagement types (survey, deliberation, assembly, budgeting)
- Structured information provision alongside questions (evidence, briefings, expert views)
- Discussion forums with moderation for constructive dialogue
- Participatory budgeting module for local spending decisions
- Citizens' assembly support with random selection and facilitation tools

**Blockers**:

- Platform limited to simple survey/consultation model
- No support for deliberative features (discussion, evidence review, iteration)
- Departments preferring quick survey results over slower deliberative processes
- Deliberative features increasing platform complexity and cost

**Related Stakeholders**: Minister (democratic legitimacy), Citizens (quality of engagement), Academic community (deliberative democracy research)

### Conflict Analysis

**Competing Drivers**:

- **Conflict 1**: Cabinet Office (SD-2) wants standardised, efficient consultation processes (quick surveys, automated analysis), while Involve/Democratic Society (SD-4) want deliberative processes (slower, richer, more resource-intensive).
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Platform supports both modes — standard consultations (survey-based, template-driven) for routine policy questions, and deliberative engagement modules (discussion, evidence review, citizens' assembly support) for major policy decisions. Clear guidance on when each mode is appropriate.

- **Conflict 2**: Departments want control over consultation design and timing, while the Cabinet Office wants standardisation and compliance with Government Consultation Principles.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Platform provides templates and guardrails (minimum consultation period, mandatory government response) while allowing departmental customisation of questions, structure, and supplementary materials.

- **Conflict 3**: Open participation risks organised lobbying campaigns distorting results, while restricting participation undermines democratic legitimacy.
  - **Resolution Strategy**: Transparency about lobbying — identify bulk submissions, weight individually crafted responses alongside organised campaigns, and publish response analysis showing both individual and campaign responses.
